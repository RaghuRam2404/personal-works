# Carousel Scripting

## 0. Source of truth (read first, every time)
Open and read `knowledge base/brand_knowledge/brand_guideline.md`.
Also read `Carousel-Only Instagram Funnel.md` (same knowledge base) for series, funnel stages, CTA ladder, and word counts.

Take every brand fact from these files: positioning, audience, enemy, mechanism, offer and prices, voice rules, words to own, banned words, privacy, proof, and visual system. Do not restate them from memory.
- If the two files conflict, the brand guideline wins.
- If the request changes a locked fact, follow it and flag the change in the output.
- If a file is unavailable, say so. Do not write from assumptions.

## 1. Intake (ask nothing unless blocked)
Infer these from the request. Ask ONE question only if the topic or audience slice is truly unclear.
- Job: diagnose / cost of drift / ritual / receipt / objection / invite.
- Funnel stage and series (from the funnel file).
- Pillar: money, muscle, or connected (prefer connected).
- Audience slice: India professional, NRI, busy parent, midlife resetter, or high-information non-doer.
- One concrete situation: a moment with a time, place, or object (Sunday night, salary credit day, Diwali week, a school fee, a desk lunch).
- One intended reader behavior: share, save, comment REP, visit profile, or join.

## 2. Angle generation
1. Write 10 hooks for the topic. Vary the pattern: contradiction, specific gap, cost, myth vs mechanism, identity line, question-of-consequence.
2. Score each hook 0-2 on five points: specific behavior, tension, reader recognition ("that is me"), curiosity gap, brand fit. Max 10.
3. Keep the top 3 and deliver the best one as the main draft. Show the other 2 as alternates.
4. Reject any hook that a generic finance or fitness page could post unchanged.

## 3. Structure
Write 8-12 slides. Never pad a short idea up to hit a higher count, and never compress a rich idea down to 8 for speed — the idea sets the count, not the other way round.

Core 8-slide skeleton (always present, in this order): hook, situation, hidden failure, cost, reframe, mechanism, one action, CTA.

Optional insert slides (add 1-4, each placed where it earns its keep, never two doing the same job):
- **Second situation beat** — right after situation: a different day or a witness reaction, when the audience needs more recognition before the turn.
- **Objection slide** — right after reframe: name and knock down the likely pushback ("this is just another spreadsheet," "I already have a tracker").
- **Split mechanism** — turn one mechanism slide into two: one beat for the money rep, one beat for the muscle rep.
- **Proof/receipt slide** — after mechanism, before one action: a real or illustrative member example.
- **Ritual slide** — after one action: the short weekly rhythm (Friday check, Sunday reset) if one action alone doesn't land the system.

Use the funnel file's per-stage structures (Reach 7-8, Problem 8, Participation 6-7, Mechanism 8-9, Proof 7, Conversion 9) as the floor, and extend toward 12 only when each added slide is a genuinely new job.

Give every slide one job and one line of thought. Each slide should end on a small open loop or a clean handoff that makes the next slide feel necessary.

## 4. Word budget
| Slide | Target words | Hard max |
|---|---|---|
| Hook | 5-10 | 12 |
| Situation | 12-20 | 25 |
| Hidden failure | 10-20 | 25 |
| Cost | 10-20 | 25 |
| Reframe | 8-18 | 25 |
| Mechanism (or each split-mechanism beat) | 15-25 | 30 |
| One action | 10-20 | 25 |
| CTA (always the last slide) | 5-12 | 15 |
| Optional: second situation beat | 10-20 | 25 |
| Optional: objection | 10-20 | 25 |
| Optional: proof/receipt | 15-25 | 30 |
| Optional: ritual | 8-15 | 20 |

- Whole carousel: about 110-170 words at 8 slides, scaling up toward 220-280 words at 12 — add words only by adding a genuinely new slide, not by loosening any single slide's budget.
- Sentences run 3-8 words. No line over 12 words.
- Caption: 40-80 words. It adds context and does not repeat the slides.
- Over budget means cut or split. Never shrink the type.

## 5. Writing craft
**Narrate, don't stack (read this first)**
Every slide must read like one person telling another person what happened, out loud, in order. It is not a list of index cards for the reader to assemble.
- Write 1-3 sentences that flow into each other. It is fine for a sentence to run onto its own line for pacing, but the lines must connect as a single thought, not sit as separate unconnected fragments.
- Join cause and effect with a plain connector: so, but, then, until, because, and. Never make the reader supply the link between two noun phrases themselves.
- Do not stack more than one bare noun-phrase fragment in a row ("Client calls. School pickup. The in-laws' visit."). If you need to list items, fold them into a sentence with a verb: "Client calls, school pickup, the in-laws' visit — each one gets a slot."
- Test: read the slide out loud exactly as written. If it sounds like someone reading a checklist instead of talking to you, rewrite it as a sentence.

**Line-level**
- Lead with the concrete noun or number, not an abstraction. "47 saved posts" beats "lots of content."
- Use the strongest word last in the sentence. That is where the green word goes.
- Use rhythm through sentence length, not through cutting sentences into disconnected shards: one short sentence, one longer one that explains it, one short turn. Example: "You knew the plan. You even saved the post. Nothing moved."
- Use one contrast per slide at most (known vs done, saved vs set up, private vs reported).
- Cut adverbs, hedges, and warm-up phrases ("Let's be honest," "The truth is").

**Slide-to-slide**
- The situation slide confirms the hook fast with a scene the reader recognizes.
- The hidden failure slide names the pattern (silent drift) without blaming the person.
- The cost slide makes the cost visible with a timeframe (a week becomes a month). Never invent statistics.
- The reframe slide flips the belief: from more information to a visible rep with a witness.
- The mechanism slide(s) show the mechanism in action, not a feature list. Use the Two-Rep Protocol and the human response.
- The one-action slide gives one action the reader can do in under 5 minutes, and holds the action, not the method.
- The final slide (CTA) asks for one thing.

**Voice**
- One adult to one adult. Dry, direct, no guru theatre.
- Use words to own from the guideline: rep, receipt, drift, visible, next rep, report, minimum week.
- Indian detail only if it sharpens the point. Do not add it as decoration.
- Diagnose the action gap. Do not prescribe investments, diets, or rehab.

**Captivating, not clinical**
Dry and direct does not mean flat or instructional. Every slide needs one small jolt: a stake, an image, or a turn the reader did not see coming.
- Give the mechanism a human stake before you explain the mechanics. Show what happens to a person, not a feature list of what the system does. "Friday: log whether the transfer ran" is a manual step; "Friday, 6 p.m. Someone asks if the transfer ran. You actually have to check" is a scene.
- Never repeat the same sentence shape twice in a row across slides ("Friday: ..." then "Same Friday: ..."). If two slides run parallel examples, give them different openers and different rhythm so neither reads like a template being filled in.
- Reach for one concrete, slightly surprising image per slide instead of a generic abstraction: a specific object, a specific time, a specific person's reaction — not "consistency" or "accountability" spoken about in the abstract.
- End on a beat that creates a small want: a question half-answered, a consequence not yet named, a status not yet revealed. That is what earns the next swipe.
- Read it back and ask: would this stop a scroll, or could it be a line from an onboarding manual? If it sounds like a manual, find the human moment inside the mechanic and lead with that instead.

## 6. Worked patterns (copy the shape, not the words)
**Weak:** "5 money habits every Indian professional should build."
**Strong:** "You saved 47 posts on investing. The transfer is still not set up."
Why: names a behavior, has a checkable gap, carries no tip list.

**Weak slide (37 words):** "Many people struggle to stay consistent with fitness because life gets busy and motivation fades, so they end up skipping workouts and forgetting their goals over time."
**Strong (11 words):** "One family function. Ten days off. Nobody asked where you went."
Why: a specific scene, a cost, a witness gap.

**Weak CTA:** "Follow for more tips!"
**Strong CTA:** "Your goals need witnesses. Join the accountability club."

**Weak (stacked fragments, reads like index cards):**
"Sunday, 9 p.m.
You block the whole week ahead.
Client calls. School pickup. The in-laws' visit.
Every name gets a slot."
**Strong (same facts, told as one thought):**
"It's Sunday, 9 p.m., and you're blocking out the whole week. Client calls, school pickup, the in-laws' visit — every one of them gets a slot on the calendar."
Why: the weak version makes the reader stitch four fragments together themselves. The strong version narrates the same scene as something one person would actually say.

## 7. Failure modes (scan for these and rewrite)
- Reads like a tip list or a mini-course.
- Reads like stacked index cards or a bullet list instead of one flowing thought (three or more bare fragments in a row with no connecting word).
- Reads like a manual or feature explainer ("Friday: log X. Same Friday: log Y.") instead of a scene with a human stake.
- Two or more slides reuse the same sentence template back to back.
- Blames laziness, culture, age, or family.
- Promises an outcome, a balance, a body, or a date.
- Only money or only muscle, with no shared system.
- The hook could belong to any account.
- A middle slide repeats the previous slide.
- The CTA is vague, or there is more than one CTA.
- Fake scarcity, fake proof, or a price that conflicts with the guideline.
- Corporate filler or motivational filler.
- Pitching inside the first 5 slides.

## 8. Self-critique loop (required before output)
1. Count the words on every slide against the budget.
2. Read every slide aloud exactly as written. If any slide sounds like a checklist instead of a person talking, rewrite it as connected sentences (see "Narrate, don't stack" in section 5).
3. Scan for repeated sentence templates and manual-style steps (see "Captivating, not clinical" in section 5). Rewrite any slide that reads like an instruction rather than a scene.
4. Read only slide 1. Would a stranger in the target audience stop? If not, swap the hook.
5. Read only slides 1 and 2 together. Does the reader want slide 3?
6. Delete the weakest slide. If nothing is lost, keep it deleted.
7. Check for banned words and phrases from the guideline, and for any claim that promises an outcome.
8. Read the CTA aloud. One ask, matched to the funnel stage.
9. Score against the quality gate. Rewrite the lowest-scoring slide once more.

**Quality gate.** Ship only if 4 of 5 pass: specific behavior; reporting or recovery involved; money, muscle, or shared system; makes the club valuable; a generic account could not post it unchanged.

## 9. Proof rules
- Real, consented, redacted proof only, in the guideline's proof order.
- Always add a timeframe and the friction.
- If no real proof exists, label it "Illustrative format."
- Never invent a member quote, result, or screenshot.

## 10. Graphics and image hand-off
- Mark one green word or phrase per slide with **bold**.
- Add a one-line "Visual note" only where the visual carries meaning (a bracket, a check, a score line, a Panda status). Apply the guideline's visual system.
- Slide 1 gets an image prompt when the funnel file's cover rule applies. Use the funnel file's prompt formula and series mood bank as the base, then apply the simplicity check below before finalizing.

**Cover image simplicity check (apply after drafting the prompt)**
- One subject, one action, nothing else in frame. If the prompt names two objects, a "setting" full of props, or a "neatly organized" anything, strip it down to the single object that carries the emotion and drop the rest.
- Background must read as near-empty: solid black or near-black (`#000000`), with generous negative space around the subject. No furniture, room, or scene detail unless it is the one subject itself.
- Exactly one accent in the brand green (`#34C363`), on one small detail of the subject only. No other color anywhere in the frame.
- No text, no logo, no Panda unless the request specifically calls for the Panda.
- Reject the prompt and rewrite it if it would need more than one sentence to describe what is in the frame.
- Before finalizing: could this image be mistaken for a busy stock photo or a lifestyle shot? If yes, remove detail until the answer is no.

## 11. Output format
1. **Brief** (one line): job / stage / series / pillar / audience slice.
2. **Hook options:** main hook plus 2 alternates, each with its score.
3. **Slides:** `Slide N - [job] - [word count]` followed by the on-slide text and any visual note.
4. **Caption** (with word count) and the CTA.
5. **Self-check:** word-budget pass, quality gate (x/5), and one line on what was cut or rewritten.
6. **Close:** category, enemy, mechanism, claim risk, CTA.
7. **Flags:** any conflict with the guideline, any changed locked fact, any claim that needs real proof.
