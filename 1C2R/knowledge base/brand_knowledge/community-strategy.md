# 1Crore2Reps Community Operating Strategy

Companion to [brand_guideline.md](./brand_guideline.md), which owns the brand promise, pricing, the Two-Rep Protocol, community-experience design (categories, gamification, pricing tiers), and voice. **This document owns execution** — how to launch, select members, onboard, run the weekly rhythm, staff human support, and measure the result. Where a brand decision is already defined in brand_guideline.md, this file links to it instead of repeating it.

**Platform note:** Skool is the current default delivery platform because of its built-in subscriptions, events, course-gating, and levels. The operating model below — paid-first launch, a minimal structure, daily human response, and the Witness System — is platform-agnostic. If a different platform (Circle, Discord + Stripe, Mighty Networks, a custom app) becomes more convenient later, remap the mechanics (categories → channels/spaces, Classroom → pinned docs, Events → a calendar tool) without changing the underlying rules. Mentions of "Skool" below describe the current implementation, not a permanent dependency.

## Core operating model

The business runs in three layers:

| Layer | Purpose | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| Public content channels (Instagram, X, etc.) | Recognition and acquisition | Show why money and fitness goals quietly disappear |
| Community platform (currently Skool) | Accountability delivery | Members declare, report, adjust, and repeat their weekly reps |
| Premium guided tier, later | Deeper human support | Smaller, timezone-matched guided cohort calls and more personal reviews |

Public content identifies the accountability problem. The community solves it. Do not give away a weaker free version of the complete community without deliberately testing that decision (see below).

## Acquisition model: paid-first by default, freemium as a tested alternative

### Default: direct-to-paid founding cohort

Launch **direct-to-paid with a small founding cohort**, not a permanent free tier. A free group creates two problems: it weakens the premium-accountability positioning, and it gives the founder two communities to operate before either is proven.

- Privately recruit the first 15–30 members, activate them, and only then promote the community publicly.
- Open the founding offer defined in brand_guideline.md (currently $29/month, capped at the first 50 members) rather than inventing a separate number here.
- Add a 14-day "fit guarantee" (did this fit your situation?), not an outcome guarantee.
- Skip a free trial initially — payment creates useful commitment from day one.
- Founding members are told they are helping refine the rituals and receive permanent founding status.

Keep low pricing aimed at genuine founding cohorts, not permanent discounting: very low pricing can attract low-commitment members, while a later price increase gives latecomers a legitimate reason to join early.

### Freemium alternative: evaluate, don't default to it

A freemium layer (public content → free community tier → paid accountability) is a legitimate model, used by many Skool businesses, but it is **not the locked 1C2R default**. Treat it as a path to test only if direct-to-paid recruitment stalls after a genuine outreach effort (see Days 8–21 in the [90-day execution plan](#ninety-day-execution-plan)).

If freemium is tested, keep these brand-aligned boundaries instead of copying a generic "free course, paid coaching" split:

| Layer | Generic freemium pattern | 1C2R boundary if tested |
|---|---|---|
| Free content | Finance/fitness tips and tutorials | Action-gap diagnosis content only — never a tip library |
| Free community tier | Knowledge, frameworks, basic training | A limited, time-bound intro to the operating system — not an indefinitely serviced free accountability club |
| Paid membership | Implementation + accountability | Visible goals, recorded progress, noticed misses, a human next action — unchanged from the locked offer |
| Premium curriculum | Full lessons behind the paywall | Rejected — the classroom stays lean regardless of tier |

The useful distinction: *free* helps a person choose their two reps; *paid* means their commitments are acknowledged, their receipts are reviewed, and their misses get a human next action. Never withhold essential instructions just to manufacture a purchase.

A brand-aligned funnel, if tested:

Public content → engagement → keyword comment or CTA → free community tier → member experiences the reporting ritual → paid invitation ("a tracker records the miss; the club notices the person") → paid onboarding → ongoing membership.

**Caveats before trusting any freemium benchmark:** conversion rates, time-to-upgrade, retention percentages, and revenue projections circulating in generic Skool growth content (e.g., "0.7% cold-traffic conversion," "3–9% free-to-paid conversion," "150 free members × 5% = paid cohort") are unverified, platform-wide claims, not 1C2R data. Do not present them to the team or in planning as forecasts — track 1C2R's own funnel metrics instead (see [Metrics & funnel tracking](#metrics--funnel-tracking)).

## Human accountability staffing plan

Paid revenue should fund the human layer rather than leaving the founder responsible for every interaction indefinitely. The service standards themselves (response times, acknowledgement SLAs) are locked in brand_guideline.md's human-touch standard — this section is about **who performs them as the community grows**.

### Staffing progression

- **Solo phase (founding cohort):** founder personally performs every role below.
- **Delegated phase (once volume exceeds what one person can answer within the SLA):** hire community managers, ideally covering different member timezones, to handle day-to-day check-ins and follow-up.
- **Founder's role after delegation:** oversee system quality, economics, and the standards below — not every individual reply.

### Role responsibilities

| Responsibility | Required behaviour |
|---|---|
| Welcome | Reply to an introduction within 12 hours |
| Commitment acknowledgement | Acknowledge every weekly commitment |
| Midweek status | Run the Wednesday green/amber/red nudge |
| Receipt review | Notice and respond to Friday reporting |
| Miss recovery | After two missed reports, send a personal nudge within 48 hours |
| Stuck member support | Ask a useful question or help identify a smaller next step |
| Monthly review | Help members review, adjust, and repeat |
| Community standards | Enforce no shame, no unsolicited prescriptions, no DM pitching |

**Do not import scope creep from generic community playbooks.** Do not add fitness form checks or financial budget reviews as staff responsibilities — the club holds members accountable to self-selected actions; it does not prescribe investments, diets, or rehabilitation. Skool has no native sub-group feature, so staff coverage is organized by response-time SLA across the one shared community, not by assigning members to pods. The only place a small, timezone-matched group genuinely exists is the optional Guided cohort tier (see brand_guideline.md pricing), capped at 6-8 members per live call.

## Member selection & admission

Do not admit everyone. One person looking for stock tips, a crash diet, or unlimited coaching can damage the culture disproportionately. Top communities screen for effort and fit rather than maximizing raw member count.

Use an application with these questions:

1. What money goal have you repeatedly postponed?
2. What fitness goal have you repeatedly restarted?
3. Why have previous attempts stopped?
4. Are you willing to report one money rep and one muscle rep every week?
5. What country and timezone are you in?
6. Are you looking for accountability, or personalized investment/medical advice?

Accept people who acknowledge that execution is their problem. Reject people seeking guaranteed results, specific investment recommendations, medical treatment, or passive access to content (consistent with "Not the audience" in brand_guideline.md).

## Platform setup: keep it minimal

Overwhelm is dangerous for 1C2R specifically: if a new member sees 40 lessons, six calls, and ten trackers, they postpone starting — the exact behavior the community is supposed to solve.

- Community categories, the classroom module list, pricing tiers, and gamification level names are already defined in brand_guideline.md's **Community experience** and **Offer architecture** sections — set the platform up to match those exactly rather than inventing a second list here.
- Start with a minimal "Start Here," a short activation sequence, and a resource vault built from real member questions — not a large prebuilt curriculum.
- Every classroom module must require an action; remove anything that exists only to make the library look bigger.

## Onboarding execution (Day 0–3)

This operationalizes the "First 48 hours" member journey defined in brand_guideline.md into a concrete day-by-day script for whoever runs onboarding.

### Day zero

The member watches the welcome video and posts:

> **My 90-day money direction:**
> **My 90-day fitness direction:**
> **What normally stops me:**
> **My timezone:**
> **Why I joined now:**

They also comment meaningfully on three member introductions.

### Day one

They publish their first Two-Rep commitment:

> **Money Rep:**
> **Muscle Rep:**
> **When each will happen:**
> **What evidence I will post:**
> **Most likely obstacle:**
> **My smaller fallback rep:**

The first quick win is not weight loss or financial growth — it is turning two private intentions into scheduled, visible commitments.

### Day two

Send a personal message:

> "Your two reps are now visible. Which one feels more likely to slip this week — and what would make it easier?"

This message must feel human, not automated.

### Day three

The member submits a green, amber, or red check-in. Anyone inactive for 48 hours receives a personal nudge. Structured early activation and a quick win in the first three days consistently correlate with better retention in community case studies.

## Weekly operating rhythm: scripts & templates

The ritual itself (Sunday Rep Plan, Monday Commitment, Wednesday Midweek Nudge, Friday Rep Receipt, Month-end Review) is defined in brand_guideline.md's **Weekly rhythm** table. Use these execution templates to run it:

### Wednesday template

> **Money Rep:** Green / Amber / Red
> **Muscle Rep:** Green / Amber / Red
> **What changed:**
> **Help needed:**
> **Next action within 24 hours:**

### Friday template

> **Money Rep:** Done / Adjusted / Missed
> **Muscle Rep:** Done / Adjusted / Missed
> **Receipt:**
> **What created or removed friction:**
> **What I will repeat or change next week:**

A miss gets the same human attention as a win. Standard response:

> "No restart. What is the smallest useful rep you can complete next?"

## Calls and events

Do not start with multiple weekly calls. Only a minority of members typically attend live calls, while searchable text discussions retain usefulness for longer. Begin with no calls or one purposeful monthly call, and add more only when demand is proven.

Start with:

- **One monthly Rep Review**, 60 minutes.
- First 15 minutes: wins and comebacks.
- Next 20 minutes: recurring money obstacles.
- Next 20 minutes: recurring fitness obstacles.
- Final 5 minutes: next month's commitment.

Answer individual questions asynchronously during the month with short voice notes, written replies, or brief screen recordings. Add a second call only when attendance and member requests justify it. (Skool Calls and recurring calendar events currently host these; any platform with scheduling works.)

## Daily operating job

Visible daily leadership and human touch are the most consistent difference between active and decaying communities. Reserve **45–60 minutes once per day** for the platform:

1. Reply to every new introduction.
2. Review posts with no responses.
3. Acknowledge submitted commitments and receipts.
4. Nudge members who have missed two reporting points.
5. Praise members who gave useful peer support.
6. Move valuable answers into the resource vault.
7. Record recurring phrases, objections, and problems for public content.

Batch the work by category rather than checking continuously: introductions first, weekly reps second, stuck questions third, receipts and wins fourth, private nudges last. Let members help one another before the founder arrives, and publicly recognize useful helpers (see the Witness System below).

Operate to the human-touch SLA defined in brand_guideline.md (12-hour intro reply, nudge after two missed reports, etc.). **If these standards cannot be maintained, pause acquisition and repair delivery before adding members** — do not scale beyond the ability to notice absence.

## Response scripts

**Completed both reps:**
> "Both receipts are in. What specifically made this week work — and can you reproduce it next week?"

**Completed only one:**
> "One receipt is real progress. What blocked the other rep: time, difficulty, environment, or unclear planning?"

**Missed both:**
> "No lecture and no restart. Choose one smaller money rep and one smaller muscle rep for the next 48 hours."

**Member disappears:**
> "You have gone quiet, which is exactly when this community is supposed to notice. Reply with only three things: green, amber, or red; what happened; your next rep."

**Member helps someone:**
> "This is 1C2R behaviour: practical help without showing off. Thank you for making another member's next rep easier."

## Member trust & incentive system (Witness System)

Skool points reward likes, not whether another person actually made a rep. Rewarding raw points invites shallow replies and reciprocal liking. The deeper trust layer is the **1C2R Witness System**, fully documented in [Community Incentive System.md](./Community%20Incentive%20System.md):

- Members earn a **Witness Receipt** when someone they helped names them in a Friday Rep Receipt (`WITNESS: @Name — what they did that helped`).
- Members who accumulate qualifying Witness Receipts across a rolling four-week window become a **Rep Witness**, earning a permanent entry on the admin-only **Witness Wall** and access to the monthly **Builder Call**.
- Anti-gaming rules, a graduated response for conduct issues, and a 90-day pilot plan are in that file — do not duplicate them here; update them there.

## Gamification & recognition

Level names and monthly recognition categories are locked in brand_guideline.md's **Gamification** subsection — use those, not a second list. In addition, tie unlocks to real progress rather than likes:

- Reporting templates.
- Monthly workshop access.
- Ability to nominate community improvements.
- Guided-cohort co-host eligibility.
- A private progress review.
- Founding council participation.

Never grant these for accumulating likes alone — tie them to the Witness System or verified receipt consistency.

## Metrics & funnel tracking

The north-star metric (Accountable Member Rate) and the full measurement framework are defined in brand_guideline.md. Track these additional **top-of-funnel metrics only if the freemium path above is actually tested**:

| Metric | What it answers |
|---|---|
| Content reach and watch time | Is the message earning attention? |
| Saves and engagement | Is the behaviour diagnosis resonating? |
| Keyword comments / link clicks | Is attention moving toward participation? |
| Free-tier join rate (if tested) | Is the entry proposition clear? |
| Free-member activation (if tested) | Do entrants actually choose and declare two reps? |
| Free-to-paid conversion (if tested) | Do people value the human layer enough to pay? |
| Time to upgrade (if tested) | How long does the decision take? |

Work toward single-digit monthly churn after the early learning period; better onboarding is the most reliable lever for reducing cancellations.

## Ninety-day execution plan

### Days 1–7

- Build the minimal platform structure.
- Record the onboarding modules.
- Create the three weekly templates.
- Write community rules.
- Create the application form.
- Create a basic sales page.
- Write the daily-response SOP.

### Days 8–21

- Personally contact 50 suitable Indians and NRIs.
- Conduct brief fit conversations.
- Accept the first 15–30 members.
- Do not run ads yet.
- Get every accepted member to post an introduction before public launch.
- If this outreach cannot fill the founding cohort, treat that as the trigger to evaluate the freemium alternative above — not before.

### Days 22–51

- Run four full accountability weeks.
- Respond personally every day.
- Hold one Rep Review.
- Interview active and inactive members.
- Record the words members use to describe their struggles.
- Collect action, consistency, and comeback proof.

### Days 52–60

- Remove unused material.
- Improve onboarding using observed drop-off points.
- Publish genuine member stories with permission.
- Create the public sales page around member language.
- Raise the new-member price toward the standard tier (see brand_guideline.md pricing).

### Days 61–90

- Begin the public content cadence.
- Add small paid retargeting only after organic content produces applications.
- Open the Guided cohort tier with timezone-matched live calls capped at 6–8 members.
- Train the first cohort co-hosts.
- Consider an annual plan only after monthly retention looks healthy.

## Do not copy blindly

Avoid these otherwise-common community-platform tactics during the first phase:

- Do not build a permanent free community by default.
- Do not create a huge course before members arrive.
- Do not launch daily calls.
- Do not reward empty engagement.
- Do not promise wealth or body outcomes.
- Do not add coaching tiers before the core membership retains people.
- Do not run ads to an empty community.
- Do not expand to more platforms because growth feels slow.
- Do not automate away the human response.
- Do not scale beyond your ability to notice absence.

## Your real work

The daily work is not primarily publishing finance and fitness information. It is:

1. **Notice:** Who stopped reporting?
2. **Respond:** What human message will bring them back?
3. **Simplify:** How can the next rep become easier?
4. **Recognise:** Which behaviour should the culture celebrate?
5. **Document:** Which member pattern should become public content?
6. **Improve:** What can make the accountability system more effective?

A minimal platform, a strict weekly rhythm, selective membership, visible founder leadership, and relentless human follow-up. The trackers, calls, and Panda graphics are support assets. The product is still: **somebody notices.**
