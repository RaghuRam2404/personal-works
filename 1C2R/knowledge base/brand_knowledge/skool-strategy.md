**Use the Skool playbook as operating guidance, but adapt it to 1Crore2Reps.** Initially, 1C2R should operate like a high-touch accountability service delivered through a community—not like a course, content library, or open discussion group.

## Core operating model

The business should have three distinct layers:

| Layer | Purpose | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| Instagram and X | Recognition and acquisition | Show why money and fitness goals quietly disappear |
| Skool community | Accountability delivery | Members declare, report, adjust, and repeat their weekly reps |
| Premium guided tier, later | Deeper human support | Smaller facilitated pods and more personal reviews |

Public content identifies the accountability problem. Skool solves it. Do not give away a weaker free version of the complete community.

## Launch paid first

For 1C2R, I recommend **direct-to-paid with a small founding cohort**, not a permanent free Skool group.

A free group would create two problems:

- It weakens the premium-accountability positioning.
- It gives you two communities to operate before one has been proven.

However, do not open the paid group to strangers while it is empty. Privately recruit the first 15–30 members, activate them, and only then promote it publicly. ## Select the right members

Do not admit everyone. One person looking for stock tips, a crash diet, or unlimited coaching can damage the culture disproportionately.

Use these application questions:

1. What money goal have you repeatedly postponed?
2. What fitness goal have you repeatedly restarted?
3. Why have previous attempts stopped?
4. Are you willing to report one money rep and one muscle rep every week?
5. What country and timezone are you in?
6. Are you looking for accountability or personalized investment/medical advice?

Accept people who acknowledge that execution is their problem. Reject people seeking guaranteed results, recommendations about specific investments, medical treatment, or passive access to content. The playbook notes that top communities actively screen for effort, fit, and likely participation rather than maximizing raw member count. ## Human-touch standard

Because accountability is the product, set measurable service standards:

- Every new introduction receives a response within 12 hours.
- Every commitment or receipt receives acknowledgement within 24 hours.
- Two missed check-ins trigger a private message.
- Every stuck post receives a question or next-action suggestion.
- Every member receives at least one direct human interaction each week.
- Automations remind; humans respond.
- No bot pretends to provide the accountability being sold.

If these standards cannot be maintained, stop acquisition temporarily and repair delivery before adding members.

## First 90 days

### Days 1–7

- Build the minimal Skool structure.
- Record the five onboarding modules.
- Create the three weekly templates.
- Write community rules.
- Create the application form.
- Create a basic sales page.
- Write the daily-response SOP.

### Days 8–21

- Personally contact 50 suitable Indians and NRIs.
- Conduct brief fit conversations.
- Accept the first 15–30 members.
- Do not run ads yet.
- Get every accepted member to post an introduction before public launch.

### Days 22–51

- Run four full accountability weeks.
- Respond personally every day.
- Hold one Rep Review.
- Interview active and inactive members.
- Record the words members use to describe their struggles.
- Collect action, consistency and comeback proof.

### Days 52–60

- Remove unused material.
- Improve onboarding using observed drop-off points.
- Publish five genuine member stories with permission.
- Create the public sales page around member language.
- Raise the new-member price from $29 to $49.

### Days 61–90

- Begin the full Instagram content cadence.
- Use X for conversation and repurposing.
- Add small paid retargeting only after organic content produces applications.
- Introduce accountability pods of 6–8 members.
- Train the first pod captains.
- Consider an annual plan only after monthly retention looks healthy.

## Metrics that matter

The main number should be the **Accountable Member Rate**:

> Percentage of paying members who report both Money and Muscle receipts in at least three out of four weeks.

Track:

- Percentage posting first two reps within 24 hours.
- Percentage submitting the first Friday receipt.
- Percentage receiving a human response within 24 hours.
- Monthly Accountable Member Rate.
- Month-one and month-three retention.
- Monthly churn.
- Cancellation reasons.
- Applications from Instagram.
- Applications from X.
- Application-to-paid conversion.
- Member referrals.
- Number of consented wins and comeback stories.

## Do not copy blindly

Avoid these otherwise-common Skool tactics during the first phase:

- Do not build a permanent free community.
- Do not create a huge course before members arrive.
- Do not launch daily calls.
- Do not reward empty engagement.
- Do not promise wealth or body outcomes.
- Do not add coaching tiers before the core membership retains people.
- Do not run ads to an empty community.
- Do not expand to more platforms because growth feels slow.
- Do not automate away the human response.
- Do not scale beyond your ability to notice absence.

## Your real work

Your job is not primarily to publish finance and fitness information.

Your daily work is to:

1. **Notice:** Who stopped reporting?
2. **Respond:** What human message will bring them back?
3. **Simplify:** How can the next rep become easier?
4. **Recognise:** Which behaviour should the culture celebrate?
5. **Document:** Which member pattern should become public content?
6. **Improve:** What can make the accountability system more effective?

That is how 1C2R should operate: **a minimal platform, a strict weekly rhythm, selective membership, visible founder leadership, and relentless human follow-up.** The PDFs, trackers, calls and Panda graphics are support assets. The product is still: **somebody notices.**
