# Home Learning Milestones
### Child: Raghu's Son | Scope: Home-based, independent of school | Prepared: May 2026

---

## How to Read This Document

- Each milestone is a single, atomic, observable output — what the child can **do, demonstrate, explain, or produce**
- Milestones are **not age-locked** — they are sequential within each domain but the pace is set by the child's readiness and journal
- Milestones are **not instructions** — they say what the destination looks like, not how to get there
- Total milestones: [MATH: 500] + [CODE: 400] + [READ: 200] + [PHYS: 210] + [FP: 128] = **1,438 milestones**

### Phase 1: Observation & Wonder (No Math)

**PHYS-001** — Can observe that a dropped object falls toward the ground regardless of its shape, and describe this observation using the word "gravity."

**PHYS-005** — Can demonstrate that a shadow grows larger when the object moves closer to the light source and smaller when it moves farther away.

**PHYS-006** — Can observe that a shadow changes shape when the angle of the light source changes, and describe this in their own words.

**PHYS-007** — Can observe that a magnet attracts iron objects and repels or attracts another magnet depending on orientation, and correctly predict attraction/repulsion before testing.

**PHYS-008** — Can sort a set of everyday objects into "magnetic" and "non-magnetic" categories and identify that only certain metals respond to magnets.

**PHYS-009** — Can demonstrate that a magnet's force acts through paper, fabric, and thin plastic without touching the attracted object.

**PHYS-010** — Can observe that ice melts into water and water evaporates, and describe these as changes of state without numerical measurement.

**PHYS-011** — Can hold an ice cube and describe what they feel (coldness, wetness, melting) and connect this to the idea that heat moves from their hand to the ice.

**PHYS-012** — Can observe that steam rises from hot water and describe the direction of heat flow using the words "hot to cold."

**PHYS-013** — Can observe that objects float or sink in water and sort a set of everyday objects by prediction before placing them in water.

**PHYS-014** — Can observe that a ball of clay sinks but the same clay shaped into a hollow boat floats, and describe in their own words why the shape matters.

**PHYS-015** — Can observe that sound travels through both air and solid surfaces (e.g., tapping a table and listening with an ear pressed to it) and describe the difference in loudness.

**PHYS-016** — Can observe that a vibrating ruler, rubber band, or tuning fork produces sound and that the sound stops when the vibration is stopped.

**PHYS-017** — Can observe that pushing or pulling an object changes its motion and describe this in terms of "force" and "movement."

**PHYS-018** — Can observe that a rolling ball slows down on its own and describe this as friction without measurement.

**PHYS-019** — Can observe that smooth surfaces cause less friction than rough surfaces by sliding the same object across both and comparing ease of movement.

**PHYS-020** — Can observe that a stretched rubber band or compressed spring stores energy by demonstrating that it can move an object when released.

**PHYS-021** — Can observe that light travels in straight lines by shining a flashlight through a small hole in a card and noting the beam's path.

**PHYS-022** — Can observe that a prism or water droplet splits white light into colors and name the visible spectrum in correct order.

**PHYS-023** — Can observe that some materials let light through (transparent), some partly (translucent), and some not at all (opaque), and sort objects accordingly.

**PHYS-024** — Can observe that warm air rises over a candle flame by placing a small piece of tissue above a lit candle (supervised) and describing the convection movement.

**PHYS-025** — Can observe that a compass needle aligns with Earth's magnetic field and consistently points north-south.

**PHYS-026** — Can observe that static electricity can attract small pieces of paper to a rubbed balloon and describe this as a force without touching.

**PHYS-027** — Can observe that a lever makes lifting easier by comparing lifting a heavy book with and without a ruler balanced on an eraser as a fulcrum.

**PHYS-028** — Can observe that a ramp (inclined plane) makes it easier to push an object to a height than to lift it straight up.

**PHYS-029** — Can observe that a pulley changes the direction of a force, demonstrating this by pulling a rope downward to lift an object upward.

**PHYS-030** — Can observe that the Moon, Sun, and stars appear to move across the sky over time and describe this in terms of Earth's rotation.

### Phase 2: Qualitative Concepts (No Equations)

**PHYS-031** — Can explain the difference between mass (amount of matter) and weight (gravitational pull on that mass) using everyday language without numbers.

**PHYS-032** — Can explain what a force is, give three examples of forces (push, pull, friction, gravity, tension), and describe what forces can do to an object (start, stop, change direction, deform).

**PHYS-033** — Can explain the concept of balanced and unbalanced forces and predict whether an object will remain still or move given a description of forces acting on it.

**PHYS-034** — Can explain what friction is, identify it as a force opposing motion, and give two examples where friction is useful and two where it is unwanted.

**PHYS-035** — Can explain why a boat floats using the concept of buoyancy — that water pushes upward on an object and this force depends on how much water is displaced.

**PHYS-036** — Can explain that objects denser than water sink and objects less dense than water float, without calculating density numerically.

**PHYS-037** — Can explain the three simple machines (lever, inclined plane, pulley) and describe what each does to a force without using equations.

**PHYS-038** — Can explain the difference between contact forces (friction, normal force, tension) and non-contact forces (gravity, magnetism, electrostatic force).

**PHYS-039** — Can explain what inertia means — that objects resist changes to their motion — and give two everyday examples.

**PHYS-040** — Can explain what energy is in qualitative terms (the capacity to do work) and give four forms: kinetic, potential, thermal, and light.

**PHYS-041** — Can explain the concept of energy transfer — that energy can move from one object to another or change from one form to another — using a ball rolling down a hill as an example.

**PHYS-042** — Can explain what a wave is qualitatively — a disturbance that carries energy without carrying matter — and give examples of sound and water waves.

**PHYS-043** — Can explain the difference between longitudinal and transverse waves using a slinky demonstration, without writing any equations.

**PHYS-044** — Can explain how sound is produced (vibrating source), how it travels (through a medium), and what happens when there is no medium (silence in a vacuum).

**PHYS-045** — Can explain why we see lightning before hearing thunder and connect this to the idea that light travels faster than sound.

**PHYS-046** — Can explain reflection of light: that light bounces off a surface at the same angle it hits it, and demonstrate this concept using a torch and a mirror.

**PHYS-047** — Can explain refraction qualitatively: that light bends when it moves from one medium to another (e.g., a straw looks bent in water) and explain why.

**PHYS-048** — Can explain what a magnetic field is qualitatively — an invisible region around a magnet where its force acts — and draw the field line pattern around a bar magnet from memory.

**PHYS-049** — Can explain what electric current is qualitatively — a flow of charges through a conductor — and distinguish between conductors and insulators using examples.

**PHYS-050** — Can explain what a complete circuit is and predict, before testing, whether a simple bulb-battery-wire circuit will light up based on whether the circuit is complete or broken.

**PHYS-051** — Can explain the difference between series and parallel circuits qualitatively, predicting what happens to other bulbs when one bulb is removed in each configuration.

**PHYS-052** — Can explain what temperature measures (average kinetic energy of particles) and what heat measures (total thermal energy transferred), and explain why they are not the same thing.

**PHYS-053** — Can explain the three modes of heat transfer — conduction, convection, radiation — and give one real-world example of each.

**PHYS-054** — Can explain what the greenhouse effect is qualitatively: that certain gases trap heat in Earth's atmosphere.

**PHYS-055** — Can explain the concept of an atom qualitatively — a nucleus containing protons and neutrons, surrounded by electrons — and identify where the positive, negative, and neutral charges reside.

**PHYS-056** — Can explain what radioactivity is qualitatively: that some nuclei are unstable and release particles or energy spontaneously, without deriving any equations.

**PHYS-057** — Can explain the difference between alpha, beta, and gamma radiation in terms of what they are and their relative penetrating power, without equations.

**PHYS-058** — Can explain what the speed of light means conceptually and why nothing with mass can reach it, in everyday language.

**PHYS-059** — Can explain what a spectrum is and distinguish between continuous spectra and line spectra, describing what each tells us about a light source.

**PHYS-060** — Can explain what pressure is qualitatively (force spread over an area) and give everyday examples of high pressure (nail tip) versus low pressure (snowshoe) without calculating.

### Phase 3: Quantitative Basics (Arithmetic-Level Math)

**PHYS-061** — Can measure the length of an object using a ruler in centimetres and millimetres, and convert between mm, cm, and m correctly.

**PHYS-062** — Can measure the mass of an object using a balance scale and record it in grams and kilograms with correct units.

**PHYS-063** — Can measure time in seconds using a stopwatch and record repeated measurements, acknowledging that measurements may vary slightly.

**PHYS-064** — Can calculate the average speed of a moving object given the total distance and total time, and state the result with correct units (e.g., m/s or km/h).

**PHYS-065** — Can use the formula speed = distance ÷ time to find any one of the three quantities given the other two, using arithmetic only.

**PHYS-066** — Can read a distance-time graph and correctly identify periods of rest (horizontal line) and constant speed (diagonal line), and estimate speed from the graph.

**PHYS-067** — Can distinguish between distance (total path length) and displacement (straight-line distance from start to end) using a simple walked path as an example.

**PHYS-068** — Can calculate the density of a regular solid object given its mass and volume (density = mass ÷ volume) using arithmetic, and state the unit (g/cm³ or kg/m³).

**PHYS-069** — Can measure the volume of an irregular solid using water displacement in a measuring cylinder, reading the meniscus correctly.

**PHYS-070** — Can calculate pressure using pressure = force ÷ area with given values in Pascals, and explain what the result means physically.

**PHYS-071** — Can calculate the weight of an object given its mass and using g = 10 N/kg (or 9.8 N/kg), and explain the difference between mass in kg and weight in N.

**PHYS-072** — Can calculate the moment (torque) of a force about a pivot using moment = force × perpendicular distance, and determine whether a see-saw is balanced.

**PHYS-073** — Can use the principle of moments to find an unknown force or distance in a balanced lever problem, showing working arithmetically.

**PHYS-074** — Can calculate the mechanical advantage of a simple machine (lever or pulley) as effort arm divided by load arm or load divided by effort.

**PHYS-075** — Can calculate work done using work = force × distance, given force in Newtons and distance in metres, and state the answer in Joules.

**PHYS-076** — Can calculate gravitational potential energy using GPE = mass × g × height, given numerical values, and state the answer in Joules.

**PHYS-077** — Can calculate kinetic energy using KE = ½ × mass × speed², given numerical values, and state the answer in Joules.

**PHYS-078** — Can apply conservation of energy to a simple pendulum or roller-coaster problem: calculate the speed at the bottom given the height at the top, using arithmetic.

**PHYS-079** — Can calculate power using power = work ÷ time, given numerical values, and state the answer in Watts.

**PHYS-080** — Can calculate frequency of a wave given the period (f = 1/T) and wave speed given frequency and wavelength (v = fλ), using arithmetic.

**PHYS-081** — Can read a thermometer in Celsius, convert Celsius to Kelvin (K = °C + 273), and explain why the Kelvin scale starts at absolute zero.

**PHYS-082** — Can calculate the heat energy absorbed or released using Q = mcΔT given specific heat capacity, mass, and temperature change in arithmetic problems.

## 5. First Principles Thinking & Independent Learning

# First Principles Thinking & Independent Learning Milestones

---

### Phase 1: Curiosity & Why-Asking

**FP-001** — After being told the answer to a factual question, spontaneously asks "but why does that work?" without any prompting.

**FP-002** — After receiving a "why" answer, asks a second "why" that goes one level deeper, without being encouraged to do so.

**FP-003** — When given a rule to follow (a math procedure, a grammar rule, a game rule), asks "where did that rule come from?" or "who decided that?"

**FP-004** — Notices that two different adults gave slightly different answers to the same question and asks which answer is right — or whether both can be.

**FP-005** — When reading or listening, pauses and says "that doesn't make sense to me" rather than silently moving on.

**FP-006** — Asks a question that is not about the thing being studied, but about something the current topic reminded him of.

**FP-007** — After an explanation, asks "is there a simpler way to say that?" or "can you show me what that looks like?"

**FP-008** — When told "that's just how it is," expresses dissatisfaction with that answer and continues probing.

**FP-009** — Spontaneously asks "what would happen if that were different?" when encountering a fact, rule, or situation.

**FP-010** — Before a session begins, brings a question he thought of on his own since the last session — not prompted by an assignment.

**FP-011** — During an unrelated activity (eating, playing, walking), says "I was thinking about X — why does it…?" carrying curiosity outside structured learning time.

**FP-012** — When shown a finished product (a map, a building, a machine), asks "how was this made?" before asking "what does it do?"

**FP-013** — After being given a definition, asks for an example that is different from the one provided, as if testing whether the definition holds in a new case.

**FP-014** — Asks "is that always true, or just usually true?" in response to a general statement.

**FP-015** — When given an answer he suspects is oversimplified, says "I think there's more to it than that" and tries to articulate what feels missing.

### Phase 2: Discovery & Self-Correction

**FP-016** — When presented with a new problem or puzzle, attempts to work it out independently before asking for help or a hint.

**FP-017** — Catches a computational or logical error in his own work before it is pointed out, and corrects it without prompting.

**FP-018** — After completing a problem, voluntarily goes back to check his own answer rather than waiting to be told whether it is correct.

**FP-019** — When two of his own answers contradict each other, notices the contradiction and tries to resolve it before moving on.

**FP-020** — Arrives at a conclusion through his own reasoning and, when shown the standard method afterward, can identify where his approach and the standard approach agree.

**FP-021** — Discovers a pattern in data or examples on his own and states the pattern before being told what to look for.

**FP-022** — When a strategy is not working, stops and explicitly tries a different approach rather than repeating the same one.

**FP-023** — After getting an answer wrong, identifies specifically where in his reasoning the error occurred rather than just correcting the final answer.

**FP-024** — Without being asked, tests a conclusion he just reached by trying it on a new example to see if it still holds.

**FP-025** — When given a worked example, covers the solution and reconstructs it himself before looking at the steps.

**FP-026** — Predicts the result of an experiment or calculation before carrying it out, then compares his prediction to the actual result without prompting.

**FP-027** — Notices that he made an assumption partway through a problem and goes back to state it explicitly before continuing.

**FP-028** — When shown a shortcut or trick, independently figures out why it works rather than just accepting and using it.

**FP-029** — After being corrected, explains the correct reasoning in his own words to confirm he understands the fix, not just the answer.

**FP-030** — Produces a working solution to a problem that is genuinely novel to him — one for which no procedure has been taught — using only tools and ideas he already possesses.

### Phase 3: Teaching & Articulation

**FP-031** — After learning something new, volunteers to explain it to another person (sibling, parent, pet, stuffed animal) in his own words without being asked.

**FP-032** — When explaining a concept, uses an analogy he invented himself rather than repeating the one he was given.

**FP-033** — When teaching back, detects a gap in his explanation mid-sentence and pauses to fill it in before continuing.

**FP-034** — When asked to explain something, starts from what he identifies as "the most basic piece" rather than starting from whatever he learned first.

**FP-035** — After explaining something, asks his listener "does that make sense?" and then adjusts his explanation based on the answer.

**FP-036** — Can explain not just what a concept is, but why it matters or what problem it solves.

**FP-037** — Identifies a part of a topic he understood well and a part he is still uncertain about, distinguishing the two clearly when asked to reflect.

**FP-038** — When explaining a process, can describe what would go wrong if one step were skipped — showing understanding of each step's purpose.

**FP-039** — Rewrites or redraws an explanation in a different format (words → diagram, diagram → words, story → numbered steps) to check his own understanding.

**FP-040** — When challenged on an explanation he gave, either successfully defends it with reasoning or updates it — rather than simply deferring to the challenger.

**FP-041** — Produces a written or spoken summary of something he studied that a younger child could understand, without being prompted to simplify.

**FP-042** — After explaining something, can identify which part of his explanation he is least confident about.

**FP-043** — When explaining a procedure, can distinguish between the steps that must happen in order and the steps where order doesn't matter.

**FP-044** — Teaches a concept back and, unprompted, includes a counterexample — something that looks like a case of the concept but isn't.

### Phase 4: Productive Struggle & Persistence

**FP-045** — When stuck on a hard problem, stays engaged for at least several minutes without asking for help or abandoning the task.

**FP-046** — When stuck, verbally describes what he has tried so far and what didn't work before asking for a hint.

**FP-047** — When given a hint, attempts to use only the hint — without asking for another — before reporting back.

**FP-048** — After failing to solve a problem, returns to it the next day or session on his own initiative rather than waiting to be redirected to it.

**FP-049** — When frustrated, can say specifically what is confusing him rather than expressing only general frustration ("I don't get it").

**FP-050** — Continues working on a problem even after making a significant error, rather than shutting down or asking to stop.

**FP-051** — When a problem feels overwhelming, independently breaks it into smaller pieces before attempting a solution.

**FP-052** — After a long struggle that ends in success, can describe what finally unlocked the solution for him.

**FP-053** — Chooses to attempt the harder version of a problem when an easier version is also available.

**FP-054** — When told an answer he worked hard for is wrong, responds by re-examining his reasoning rather than expressing that the effort was wasted.

**FP-055** — Sits with an open question — one without a known answer — and continues thinking about it across multiple sessions without demanding resolution.

**FP-056** — When a new approach is suggested, tries it genuinely rather than abandoning the attempt after one difficulty.

**FP-057** — After struggling unsuccessfully, can articulate at least one thing he now knows that he didn't know before the attempt.

**FP-058** — When given a problem with no solution, identifies that no solution exists and explains why, rather than producing a forced or incorrect answer.

### Phase 5: Cross-Domain Pattern Recognition

**FP-059** — Notices that a structure, rule, or pattern from one subject appears in a different subject, and states the connection unprompted.

**FP-060** — When learning something new, spontaneously says "this is like what we did in [other subject]" and explains the similarity.

**FP-063** — Recognizes when a concept from everyday life (fairness, balance, cause-and-effect) is the same idea that underlies a formal topic being studied.

**FP-064** — After encountering a concept in one context, independently looks for other contexts where it might apply, without being prompted.

**FP-065** — Notices when a new topic violates a pattern he expected from another domain, and asks why the exception exists.

**FP-066** — Identifies a shared underlying unit or primitive between two different systems (e.g., a smallest indivisible element in language and in matter) without being told to compare them.

**FP-067** — When building a mental model of something new, borrows structural vocabulary from a domain he already understands and uses it accurately.

**FP-068** — Identifies an analogy that breaks down at some point and articulates specifically where and why it stops working.

**FP-069** — Recognizes that a disagreement between two people is actually caused by each using the same word to mean different things, and names the ambiguity.

**FP-070** — Connects a historical event to a present-day situation by identifying the underlying dynamic that both share, not just the surface similarities.

**FP-071** — Notices a feedback loop (a cycle where an output becomes an input) in a real-life or studied system, and names it without the vocabulary being introduced.

**FP-072** — After studying two different methods for achieving the same result, identifies what trade-offs each makes and what conditions would make each preferable.

### Phase 6: Self-Directed Learning

## Revision Framework

### What Revision Means in This System

There are three distinct things revision must accomplish — and they are not the same:

1. **Consolidation** — the concept settles into long-term memory through sleep and spaced re-encounter. You create conditions for it; you don't force it.
2. **Fluency** — the concept becomes automatic. A calculation that once took 3 minutes takes 5 seconds. A code pattern that once required thinking is written without hesitation. This is repeated low-friction use until the concept is muscle memory.
3. **Transfer** — the real test of whether knowledge is truly owned. Can he apply a concept inside a problem from a different domain, or in a context he has never seen? Transfer is proof that knowledge is not brittle.

All three layers below target one of these.

---

### Layer 1 — Embedded Re-Encounter (Every Session)

Targets: Consolidation + Fluency

Every session opens with a **3–5 minute warm-up** that uses 2–3 already-achieved milestones — not as review, but as the natural entry point into that session's new work. The old knowledge is exercised because the new thing requires it. This is never called "revision" and never feels like going backwards.

**How to choose what to embed:**
- Pick milestones from the same domain as the session's primary focus
- Pick ones that are prerequisites for the current milestone being worked on
- Vary the surface: a different number, a different object, a different scenario — same underlying concept

**What this is NOT:**
- Not re-explaining a concept
- Not asking "do you remember X?"
- Not a quiz

---

### Layer 2 — Revisit Queue (Session-Triggered + Journal Override)

Targets: Consolidation + Transfer

#### The Mechanical Rule
Any milestone that has not been **actively used in 3 sessions** (counted in sessions, not calendar days) automatically enters the revisit queue. Session count resets when the milestone is used — even informally, even briefly.

#### The Journal Override
When writing the weekly journal entry, the parent can:
- **Push early** — add a milestone to the queue before the 3-session threshold, if it felt shaky during a session even though it was recently used
- **Defer** — remove a milestone from the queue that the rule flagged, if the parent observed it being used organically (during play, explanation to someone else, or an unrecorded context) and it felt solid

#### How Revisit Sessions Work
Revisit is always **transfer-tested**, never recognition-tested.

| Wrong (recognition) | Right (transfer) |
|---|---|
| Re-demonstrate the same type of problem | Apply the concept in a new domain context |
| "Do you remember how to do X?" | Present a scenario that requires X, without naming X |
| Repeat the original worked example | Combine with 1–2 other achieved milestones in a new problem |

A milestone exits the queue when it is successfully demonstrated in a transfer context — not when it is re-demonstrated in the original context.

#### Queue Management
- Maximum queue size per domain: **5 milestones**
- If the queue exceeds 5 for any domain, pause new milestone work in that domain until the queue is cleared
- Queue across all domains is checked at the start of each weekly journal entry

---

### Layer 3 — Integration Challenge (Every 10 Milestones in a Domain)

Targets: Transfer + proof that milestones are connected, not isolated

After every **10 milestones achieved in a domain**, dedicate one full session to a single **integration challenge** — a problem or task the child has never seen that requires combining multiple achieved milestones simultaneously.

**Rules for integration challenges:**
- The problem cannot be solved using any single milestone alone
- The problem must require at least 3 previously-achieved milestones from the domain
- The parent does not name which concepts are needed — the child must identify the tools
- If the child cannot complete it, this is diagnostic: it reveals which milestones are held in isolation and need bridging
- The challenge is not marked pass/fail — it is observed and journaled

**Examples of what this looks like:**
- Math: after MATH-001–010, present a sorting + ordering + counting challenge that requires all three skills together in one scenario
- Code: after CODE-031–040 (ScratchJr), ask for a mini animated scene with movement, sound, a wait, and a repeat — without naming each block
- Physics: after PHYS-061–070, give a real-world estimation problem that requires speed, density, and pressure concepts together

---

### Revision Priority Order

When multiple layers are active at once (Layer 2 queue has items AND a Layer 3 challenge is due), priority is:

1. Layer 3 Integration Challenge (due every 10 milestones — overrides everything)
2. Layer 2 Queue clearance (if queue is at 5 — pause new work)
3. Layer 1 Warm-up (every session, always)
4. New milestone work

## Cross-Domain Dependency Map

### How to Use This Map

Inside each domain, milestones are always sequential — work through them in order.

Across domains, this map defines the **hard gates**: specific milestones in one domain that cannot be meaningfully attempted until a specific milestone in another domain is achieved. "Hard" means structurally required — not "it would help," but "it literally cannot be done without it."

**Practical rule:** Before starting any milestone flagged in this map, check whether its listed prerequisite is already marked achieved in the other domain. If not, that prerequisite goes to the top of that domain's session queue.

FP milestones have no hard cross-domain prerequisites — they are woven into every session regardless of domain.

### Section 4 — Code ↔ Physics Hard Gates

This cluster is deliberately short — most Code and Physics milestones are independent of each other.

### Physics milestones requiring Code prerequisites

| Physics Milestone | Requires (Code) | Why it's a hard gate |

| — | — | — |

*No Physics milestones in this system have a Code hard gate.*

All physics milestones from PHYS-001 through PHYS-210 are framed as observe, explain, derive, calculate, draw, or read — none require running or writing code to complete. Even the most computation-heavy milestones (PHYS-175 radioactive decay law, PHYS-195 Schrödinger equation for particle in a box, PHYS-199 Lagrangian mechanics) are solved analytically by hand or with a scientific calculator. PHYS-210 (arXiv paper reading) involves reading papers that may describe computational methods, but the milestone itself requires comprehension and note-taking, not code execution. If the milestone system later adds explicitly computational milestones (e.g., "numerically solve the Schrödinger equation for a harmonic oscillator in Python"), those would create hard gates on Python fundamentals (CODE-099–150).
