Physicists expected brightness to matter.
But brightness had nothing to do with it.

Every single time.

🤔 What determines whether electrons escape?

What Experiments Showed

Three clues that broke
classical physics

These three observations had no explanation — until 1905.

1
🎚️
Threshold Frequency
Each metal has a cut-off frequency. Below it — nothing, no matter how bright. Above it — electrons always fly out.

Wave theory says: any color should work if bright enough.
Reality: Nope.
2
⚡
Instantaneous
Electrons appear in less than 0.000000001 seconds — even from incredibly faint light.

Wave theory says: faint light needs time to "charge up" an electron.
Reality: Instant. No delay.
3
🎯
Frequency, Not Brightness
Electrons fly out faster when the light is more blue/violet — not when it's brighter.

Wave theory says: brighter light → faster electrons.
Reality: Frequency determines speed.
▲ Sodium metal threshold: green light (~5.5 × 10¹⁴ Hz). Red never works. UV always works.

Classical Physics · 1800s

Why the Wave Theory
was confidently wrong

🌊
Wave Theory Predicted
Energy spreads as a continuous wave. More brightness = more energy. Any color should eventually work if you wait long enough or use bright enough light.
🔬
What Actually Happened
Red light on zinc? Nothing. Ever. Faint UV on zinc? Electrons immediately. Bright red × 10 hours? Still nothing. The wave theory was completely wrong.
💡
The Conclusion
Light cannot be a continuous wave that accumulates energy slowly. Something else is going on. Einstein figured it out in 1905.
▼
Einstein · 1905

"Light is made of
packets of energy"

Einstein proposed that light doesn't spread out as a smooth wave.
It comes in discrete chunks called photons.

Each photon's energy
E = hf
h = Planck's constant  |  f = frequency
Higher frequency =
More energetic photon
Red photon: weak  →  UV photon: powerful

How It All Fits Together

The vending machine
analogy

Every electron in a metal needs a minimum energy to escape — called the work function (φ). Think of it as the price of admission.

Coin too small (hf < φ)
Photon energy < work function.
Electron stays put. No ejection.
Exact price (hf = φ)
Photon just barely pays the price.
Electron escapes with zero KE.
More than enough (hf > φ)
Leftover energy becomes speed.
KE = hf − φ  ✓

Why This Matters

You use this
every day

The photoelectric effect isn't just a physics curiosity.
It's the operating principle behind technology all around you.

☀️
Solar Panels
Photons from sunlight knock electrons loose in silicon. Those electrons flow as electric current. Your phone charges from this.
📸
Digital Cameras
Each pixel in a camera sensor is a tiny metal surface. Photons hit it → electrons → the image is recorded. Every photo uses this.
🚪
Automatic Doors
A light beam crosses the doorway. When you walk through, photons are blocked → fewer electrons → current drops → door opens.
🏥
Medical Scanners
X-ray detectors use the photoelectric effect. High-energy photons knock electrons from detector material → creates the image.
