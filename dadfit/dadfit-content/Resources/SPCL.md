## The SPCL Framework

The promise that I have for you today is that I will show you a framework that I have called SPCL, like special if you want, but it's how to build influence rather than how to get as many views as possible or anything like that. It's a four-part framework and I want to break down each of the four components for you so that you can think about how you want to approach making content and building a brand in general.

SPCL stands for:
- **S** - Status
- **P** - Power
- **C** - Credibility
- **L** - Likeness

### Why Stack All Four Elements

What's interesting about all four of these elements is that they all can work independently, but the idea is that you want all four to be stacked together. This is what gives you the most influence. Any of these four on their own would give you influence, but if you have all four, that's when you become super influential.

## 1. Status (S)

### Defining Status Operationally

Status is someone who controls reinforcers in a given environment. For those of you who are new to the channel, I like to operationalize things. Meaning like I like to look at objective reality and describe how you would see it with your eyes rather than try and put a whole bunch of emotional words around stuff because that type of language is what confused me for a very long time when I was coming up. 

Fundamentally, if you control the good stuff that people want, then you will have status no matter what it is.

### The Bartender Example

The simplest example I have is like if you go to a bar and it's a busy bar and there's a bartender and you have to get the bartender attention in order to get a drink or booze, that guy in that moment has status. He controls a scarce resource. 

But if that guy walks out of the bar no longer controlling that scarce resource, he does not have the same status or even close to it. Outside of the bar, he's not getting tips every single 5 seconds when he moves his hands a little bit and says something nice. People aren't waving money at him as he walks in the street. Of course not. It's because he has status in one condition and he doesn't have another.

### The Trust Fund Kid vs. The Crypto Expert

If a kid inherits money, they're going to have status. If you have money, even if you didn't earn it, even if you didn't do anything, if you have money, you will have some degree of status because you control something other people want. Period. That's how it works. 

But would that kid who has money have the same status as a kid who has money who also gave you 10 different cryptocurrency picks that all popped off? Well, if he gave you 10 different picks and you followed them and they all popped off, how much more influence would that person have over them saying, "Hey, you should put money in this." Think about how much more influence version two would have with just two of those things versus version one. That's the difference.

### Key Takeaway

Status = You control scarce resources

## 2. Power (P)

### Most Important Element

I would say if I had to only pick one, I would pick power. And I'll explain why.

### Say-Do Correspondence

Power comes from something in the behavioral dynamics world called say-do correspondence. What that means is if I say something and then you do it and then a good thing occurs, a reinforcing event happens afterwards, you are more likely to comply with a following request.

So said differently: I gave you the example of the guy who says, "Hey, here's 10 stock picks." You buy them and then a good thing happens. The thing goes up. Great. So that person has status AND power, which is why they are more influential than the trust fund kid who just has money.

### How Events Can Check Multiple Boxes

What makes this a little bit more muddied is that sometimes one event can check multiple boxes. When we launched the book we did 100 million plus in sales. That in and of itself, me having money from the event gives me status. Me saying, "Hey, you can launch stuff in this way" gives me credibility, because I show that I have an event, something that has happened as a result of me doing it. 

And then if I give people directions on how they can do things that are similar and then good things happen, then all of a sudden you get power.

### Key Takeaway

Power = Say-do correspondence (you give advice → they follow → good things happen)

## 3. Credibility (C)

### Third Party Validation

Credibility is the third party stuff. There's something that you can observe with your eyes. The reason that my ads do well when I have my $10 million building behind me is like, oh, well, that's hard to fake. So you have credibility there.

One event like selling a company can give you money. It can give you credibility.

### The Guinness Example

The reason that I had at my launch, for example, I had Guinness—I had to pay those judges to be on site—was because I wanted to validate that the books that we did and the revenue that we generated was legit. So I had a third party that most people respect as a legitimate corporation whose entire business is based on trust that they validate and verify proof that these records were broken. And so that gives credibility.

### Key Takeaway

Credibility = Third party validation of your results

## 4. Likeness (L)

### Relatability

Likeness is what some people say relatability. This can be both psychological in terms of you share similar values with this person—you like their vibe, whatever you want to say. You like their behavior set and that behavior set matches to people who have been positive in your life in the past. Or they literally just look like you.

### Gender Example

Leila and I could talk about the exact same stuff, but she's a girl and so she's going to have more chicks who follow her than me. For the few ladies who do follow me, I appreciate you. I really do. But I have like an 89% male audience. Leila, I think, is like 54% female and she talks about almost exclusively money and business, obviously with some mindset stuff too.

### Just Be You

Likeness, I think so much more of it is just like just be you. There's zero ROI in trying to be or act in a way that is different than who you are. It's a relatively trite message, but like most people are NPCs. Most people say pre-recorded scripts. They look at the four different outfit combinations that exist for different mental stereotypes.

### Finding Your Uniqueness

I think what makes you unique is if you actually lean into the nuances that make you you and actually have a way to defend why you do what you do. Because most people don't even think about why they do what they do. And if you do things and you don't know why, it's because you're following someone else's directions for your life rather than your own.

### Key Takeaway

Likeness = Be authentically you + share similar values/appearance
