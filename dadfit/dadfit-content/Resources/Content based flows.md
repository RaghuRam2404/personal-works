# DadFit Hook Strategy: Content-Type Frameworks with Narrative Flow

## Overview

Your hook strategy operates across three content types, each with a specific narrative flow and structural template:

- **Educational**: Problem → Solution → CTA (teach how to solve)
- **Storytelling**: Exposition → Rising Action → Climax → Resolution (build emotional connection)
- **Authority**: Before → After → Process → CTA (demonstrate proof)

Each type leverages the same psychological principles (pattern interruption, polarization, value delivery, tribal belonging) but structures them differently based on audience psychology. This document maps your 9 hook concepts + 4 value pillars to each content type with actionable templates for immediate implementation.

---

## I. EDUCATIONAL CONTENT: Problem → Solution → CTA

**Purpose**: Teach problem-solving, deliver actionable frameworks, build authority through knowledge.

**Narrative Flow**: Identify specific pain → reveal the solution → direct viewer to action

**Psychological Driver**: Curiosity gap + pattern interrupt + value delivery

**Retention Target**: ~50% hold-through (viewers stay to learn the solution)



### Template 1: Myth-Busting Framework

**Flow**: Problem Identification → Solution Explanation → Action

**Section 1: Problem (0-5 seconds)**
Identify the false belief causing viewer failure.

**Structure**: 
- State the myth people believe
- Show consequence of believing it
- Create pattern interrupt with "Actually..." or "Wrong."

**DadFit Examples**:
- "Everyone says you need 8 glasses of water daily. That's wrong for dads in deficits."
- "Your trainer told you high volume builds muscle. Meanwhile, you're injured and weak."


**Section 2: Solution (5-20 seconds)**
Break the myth with evidence and deliver the truth.

**Structure**:
- State the truth with specificity
- Show mechanism (why this is true)
- Provide actionable insight

**DadFit Examples**:
- "Truth: You need 0.7-0.8g protein per pound in a surplus. That's it. Here's why the mechanism matters: [explain]"
- "Truth: 3-4 quality training days outperform 6 mediocre ones. Progressive overload beats volume. Here's the periodization: [show]"


**Section 3: CTA (20-30 seconds)**
Direct specific action.

**DadFit Examples**:
- "Save this. Calculate your actual protein needs using this formula. Tag a dad who needs this."
- "Follow for the full periodization breakdown coming tomorrow."

### Template 2: Step-by-Step Framework

**Flow**: Problem Identification → Framework Delivery → Application CTA

**Section 1: Problem (0-4 seconds)**
Open with question or problem statement.

**Structure**:
- Question hook that forces mental participation
- Or: State the problem others face

**DadFit Examples**:
- "How many of you were told THIS about meal timing?" [pause for mental response]
- "Struggling to lose belly fat despite morning walks?"


**Section 2: Solution Framework (4-22 seconds)**
Deliver the system step-by-step.

**Structure**:
- Break framework into 3-4 distinct parts (numbered or sequential)
- Use visual changes every 2-3 seconds
- Show what to do, not just concept

**DadFit Examples**:

*Example A: Progressive Overload Framework*
- "Step 1: Select weight you can lift for 8 reps with good form"
- "Step 2: Once you hit 10 reps, increase weight by 2-5%"
- "Step 3: Repeat until you hit strength target"
- "That's progressive overload. Systems beat motivation."

*Example B: Meal Prep System*
- "Step 1: Cook 3 proteins (chicken, ground turkey, fish)"
- "Step 2: Prep 2 carb sources (rice, potatoes)"
- "Step 3: Choose 3 vegetables, add to containers"
- "15 minutes, 6 meals, 3 days of eating solved."


**Section 3: CTA (22-30 seconds)**
Direct action tied to framework.

**DadFit Examples**:
- "Save this framework. Use it Monday. Comment 'SYSTEM' and I'll send you the tracking sheet."
- "Download the full meal prep template in bio—it includes shopping list."

## II. STORYTELLING CONTENT: Exposition → Rising Action → Climax → Resolution

**Purpose**: Build emotional connection, establish relatability, create tribal belonging.

**Narrative Flow**: Set the scene → build tension/stakes → peak moment of transformation → show the outcome

**Psychological Driver**: Identity + emotional arc + aspiration

**Retention Target**: ~65% hold-through (stories hold better than education—people stay for emotional payoff)

---

### Template 1: Hero's Journey / Transformation Arc

**Flow**: Exposition (who I was) → Rising Action (challenge/struggle) → Climax (breakthrough moment) → Resolution (who I am now)

**Section 1: Exposition (0-5 seconds)**
Introduce yourself and the relatable "before" state.

**Visual + Text**:
- Show yourself before, or describe state vividly
- Make it deeply relatable to target audience
- Create identification ("That's me")

**DadFit Examples**:
- "I was a chubby programmer. I wanted to be strong. But I didn't know how."
- "I was a soft dad. My kids would ask me to play tag and I'd be breathless. I hated it."


**Section 2: Rising Action (5-15 seconds)**
Build tension: What blocked you? Why was it hard? Make the struggle specific.

**Visual + Text**:
- Show the wrong approach, mistake, or challenge
- Make emotions clear (frustration, shame, confusion)
- Create investment in the problem

**DadFit Examples**:
- "I trained like a 20-year-old gym bro: 6 days/week, high volume, no recovery plan. By month 2, my shoulders were destroyed and my lower back was inflamed. I thought that's just how it works."
- "I tried keto. Low-fat. Intermittent fasting. Calorie counting. Every time, I'd start strict, then life happened, and I'd feel like a failure. I accepted I was just weak-willed."


**Section 3: Climax (15-22 seconds)**
The breakthrough moment. When did things change? What realization shifted everything?

**Visual + Text**:
- Show the insight, realization, or decision point
- Make it emotional (this is the peak)
- Connect it to a principle or system

**DadFit Examples**:
- "Then I realized: Recovery is where adaptation happens. So I switched to 3x/week training with real intensity and proper recovery. Within 8 weeks, I was stronger and healthier. Everything changed."
- "Then I realized: Motivation is a feeling that fades. I needed a system. So I built the 3-number nutrition system instead. No willpower needed. Just follow the numbers."


**Section 4: Resolution (22-30 seconds)**
Show the outcome and the invitation.

**Visual + Text**:
- Show yourself now (transformed)
- Provide specific metric or transformation proof
- Invite viewer to join journey

**DadFit Examples**:
- "Now at 38, I'm the strongest I've ever been. I play tag with my kids without getting breathless. This is what changed. This is what I teach now."
- "Now I've kept weight off for 3 years. I don't count calories. I hit my 3 numbers. I feel like I finally have control. That's why I built DadFit."

## III. AUTHORITY CONTENT: Before → After → Process → CTA

**Purpose**: Demonstrate proof through results, social proof, and methodology. Show "I know what I'm doing."

**Narrative Flow**: Establish the starting point → show the transformation → explain the process/system → direct to next step

**Psychological Driver**: Credibility (removes risk) + aspirational proof + system repeatability

**Retention Target**: ~60% hold-through (people stay to understand how results were achieved)

## VI. Appendix: The Psychology Behind Each Flow

**Educational (Problem → Solution → CTA)**: Taps into curiosity and practical value. Viewers stay because they want the solution. CTA converts because they've already received value and trust you.

**Storytelling (Exposition → Rising Action → Climax → Resolution)**: Taps into identity and emotional connection. Viewers stay because they're invested in the outcome. Resolution creates emotional payoff that makes them want to follow your journey.

**Authority (Before → After → Process → CTA)**: Taps into credibility and aspiration. Viewers stay because they want to know "how" you did it. Process explanation removes perceived risk. CTA converts because they see it's achievable.
