### Defining Fitness

Fitness has no single definition — it varies by sport:

- **Cricket:** Explosive power, running ability, sustained performance under fatigue
- **Marathon:** Running long distances without tiring
- **Strongman:** Extreme power to lift and throw massive weights — but can't run fast

**In DadFit**, the targets are:

1. **Functional strength** — lift a sofa, carry a 20 kg water can without injury
2. **Endurance** — walk long distances carrying kids or luggage, sprint when needed
3. **Pain-free movement** — no lower back pain, shoulder pain, or knee pain
4. **Low visceral fat**
5. **Achievable without affecting work and family**

| Metric | What It Measures |

## Video 2: Diet

### The First Law of Thermodynamics

Our body works like a machine — it needs the right fuel in the right quantities. Energy can neither be created nor destroyed. Excess weight in the body must be converted to another form (sweat and carbon dioxide). **That's the only way to lose weight.** No shortcuts.

Each food has energy measured in **calories**. When total calorie intake is less than calories spent, it results in **caloric deficit (negative energy balance)** = weight loss.

---

### Macronutrients

Food has two types of nutrients: **macro** and **micro**. Let's go one by one.

#### Protein — 4 cal/g

Protein is the most important macro for muscle building. It also has a **high thermogenic effect** — your body spends more energy to digest it, keeping you fuller longer.

**Amino acids** are the building blocks of protein. Of 20 amino acids, 11 are non-essential and **9 are essential** (your body can't produce them). Among those, **leucine** plays the biggest role in muscle repair and growth.

**Veg vs Non-Veg Protein:**

| Factor | Non-Veg | Veg |
|---|---|---|
| Amino acid profile | Complete (all 9 EAAs) | Often incomplete — need to combine (e.g., dal + roti for methionine) |
| Bioavailability | Higher — 100g chicken breast → 21g absorbed | Lower — 100g rajma has 22g protein but only 15–16g absorbed |
| Fiber | Low — include separately | Higher — better for digestion |

**Veg protein sources** (per 100g raw): Soya chunks (52g), whey protein, paneer, lentils (dal), rajma, chana

**Non-veg protein sources** (per 100g raw): Chicken breast (21g, low fat), goat meat (20g, more fat), fish (varies), eggs (6g/egg, includes yolk vitamins)

**Daily protein target:**
- WHO recommendation (sedentary): 0.8g/kg body weight
- Bodybuilders: 2.2g/kg
- **DadFit recommendation: 1.5–1.75g/kg** → for 65 kg = **100–115g/day**

---

#### Fat — 9 cal/g

Fat is essential — it's required for brain function, hormone production, and absorbing vitamins A, D, E, and K. Include it in calculated amounts.

**Types of fat:**

| Type | Sources | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| **Saturated fat** | Meat, butter, ghee | Fine in calculated amounts |
| **Unsaturated fat** | Nuts, seeds, avocado | Good fat — include regularly |
| **Trans fat** | Processed food, fried items | **Avoid entirely** — raises LDL, lowers HDL |

**Good fat sources:**
- Nuts: almonds, cashews, walnuts (eat measured, not by the handful)
- Seeds: flaxseeds, chia seeds, hemp seeds, sunflower seeds (also have fiber)
- **Ghee** — ideal for cooking at high temperature; best daily fat source
- Full-fat dairy: cheese, full-fat milk
- Cold-pressed oils: sesame or coconut oil for cooking

**Healthy cooking methods:** Air frying, grilling, steaming, sautéing with measured oil (1–2 tbsp)

**Avoid:** Deep frying, refined oils

---

#### Carbs — 4 cal/g

Carbs are the go-to energy source. The body converts carbs to glucose; unused glucose is stored in muscles and liver as **glycogen**. Excess glycogen is stored as **fat**.

**Simple vs Complex Carbs:**

| Type | Breakdown | Blood Sugar | Examples |
|---|---|---|---|
| Simple | Fast | Quick spike and crash | Sweets, sugar, white rice |
| Complex | Slow | Sustained energy | Brown rice, oats, quinoa, millet, barley, sweet potato |

**Fiber (part of carbs):**

| Type | Function | Sources |
|---|---|---|
| **Soluble** | Dissolves in water, forms gel — lowers cholesterol and blood glucose | Oats, nuts, seeds |
| **Insoluble** | Promotes bowel movement, prevents constipation | Vegetables, fruit skins |

#### Gut Health

The gut (mouth to rectum) contains **trillions of bacteria — the gut microbiome**. They affect immunity, health, and mood. To keep the gut healthy, include:

- **Prebiotics** — feed existing gut bacteria: onions, garlic, banana, oats
- **Probiotics** — live microorganisms from fermented foods: curd, yogurt, kefir, kimchi, sauerkraut

> **Self-learn:** Glycemic index vs glycemic load | FODMAP vegetables | Starchy vs non-starchy vegetables

---

### Micronutrients

Measured in milligrams/micrograms. Vitamins and minerals help the body perform optimally at the cellular level.

**Vitamins:**

| Type | Needs | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| Fat-soluble | Fat to absorb | A, D, E, K |
| Water-soluble | Water to absorb | B complex, C |

**Common deficiencies in modern diet:** Vitamin D, B12, iron, zinc, magnesium

**Best food sources for micronutrients:** Whole eggs, organ meats (mutton liver/spleen), fatty fish, nuts, seeds, dairy, fruits, and vegetables

---

### There Is No "Weight Loss Food"

Weight loss = caloric deficit. Weight gain = caloric surplus. **There is no magical fat-loss food.**

The difference is **food quality**:

- Air-fried home-cut potato fries (boiled first to remove starch, air-fried with salt/pepper) ✓
- Deep-fried French fries ✗

Same potato. Different quality. Choose better.

---

### Supplements

Supplements fill gaps in your diet — they don't replace it. Safe, well-researched options:

| Supplement | Purpose | Dosage |
|---|---|---|
| **Whey protein** | Fill protein gap — 24–30g protein/scoop, fast absorbing | As needed; use isolate if lactose intolerant |
| **Creatine monohydrate** | Muscle building, brain function — adds water to muscle for better lifts | 3–5g/day, same time daily |
| **Multivitamin** | Insurance against missed micronutrients | With a fat-containing meal |
| **Fish oil** | Brain and heart health (good fat) | Start with 300mg, build to 1g/day |
| **Vitamin D3** | Compensate for lack of sunlight | As needed |
| **Magnesium glycinate** | Muscle recovery, stress management, better sleep | As directed |
| **Collagen** | Skin and connective tissue health | As directed |
| **Pre-workout** | Caffeine-based energy and focus boost | 30–45 min before workout; use in moderation, not daily |

> For supplements like L-carnitine, Ashwagandha, etc. — consult a good **endocrinologist** with a proper blood test first.

### TDEE: How Many Calories Per Day?

**TDEE = Total Daily Energy Expenditure** (in calories). It has four components:

| Component | Description |
|---|---|
| **BMR** (Basal Metabolic Rate) | Calories to keep you alive at rest; higher with more muscle |
| **EAT** (Exercise Activity Thermogenesis) | Calories burned during gym sessions |
| **NEAT** (Non-Exercise Activity Thermogenesis) | Calories from non-planned activity (walking, playing with kids) |
| **TEF** (Thermic Effect of Food) | Calories to digest food; protein has the highest TEF |

**Maintenance calories formula:**
`Body weight (kg) × 2.2 × 14`

**Example:** 65 kg × 2.2 × 14 = **~2000 calories/day**

#### Macro Split (for 2000 cal, 65 kg person)

| Macro | Target | Grams | Calories |
|---|---|---|---|
| Protein | 1.75g/kg × 65 = 114g | 114g | 456 cal |
| Fat | 30% of total calories | 67g | 600 cal |
| Carbs | Remaining calories | 236g | 944 cal |

Split across 3 meals. Choose your preferred sources for each macro.

---

### Eating Guidelines

1. **Always prioritize protein** — 1.5–1.75g/kg/day
2. Include good healthy fats
3. Include good fiber
4. Go for complex carbs
5. **No between-meal snacks** — let your body digest meals fully
6. **Drink plenty of water** — start the day with 400–500ml of warm water

---

### How to Lose Weight

**Target:** 1–2% of body weight per month (sustained, not fast)

**Example for 90 kg person:**
- Maintenance calories: 90 × 2.2 × 14 = 2,772 cal/day
- 2% weight loss target: 1.8 kg/month
- 1 kg loss requires 7,700 cal deficit
- 1.8 kg = 13,860 cal deficit over 30 days = **462 cal/day deficit**
- **Eat 2,310 calories/day** to lose at target pace

> **Self-learn:** Why does 7,700 caloric deficit = 1 kg of weight loss?

### Tracking Food

Invest in a **digital food weighing scale** and measuring spoons. Log food in apps like **HealthifyMe** or **MyFitnessPal**.

- Track in grams or tablespoons — not "a cup of rice" or "100 ml of oil"
- Regular tracking trains your instinct to eyeball calories even when you can't log

---

### Reading Grocery Labels

**Ignore the front of the pack — it's marketing. Read the back.**

Check:
1. **Macros** — protein, fat, added sugars, trans fat, fiber, sodium
2. **Ingredients** — listed by descending weight

**Avoid products with:** maltodextrin, high fructose corn syrup, palm oil, excess chemicals

When in doubt: buy it, cook it, eat it for a week, observe how you feel and your energy levels, then decide.

---

### Meal Timing

- **Eat within an 8–10 hour window** each day — remaining hours are fasting
- Fasting allows the body to heal
- You can aim for one **24-hour fast per week** for additional recovery
- **Don't eat 2–3 hours before sleep** — let the body concentrate on rest, not digestion

---

### Alcohol and Cigarettes

**Avoid both.** They:
- Impair muscle building
- Damage internal organs irreversibly
- Cause dehydration
- Disrupt sleep

---

### Diet Implementation Path

Follow this top to bottom. Master one before moving to the next:

1. Drink enough water; remove junk food from home (~1 week)
2. Avoid alcohol and cigarettes
3. Track food with a weighing scale
4. Hit protein targets daily
5. Transition to complex carbs and good fats
6. Time meals within an 8–10 hour window

---

### Cheat Meals

Two variables to consider when facing a tempting food:

1. **Joy of eating** — how much joy does it bring?
2. **Guilt of eating** — how much will you regret it?

If joy > guilt → eat it, but not too often.
If guilt >= joy → skip it.

No rigid rules. Think, take a decision, move on.

## Video 3: Exercise

### Resistance Training vs. Cardio

Both are important and complementary:

| Type | Benefits |
|---|---|
| **Resistance training** | Builds muscle mass, improves bone strength, boosts metabolism |
| **Cardio** | Improves heart and lung health, burns calories, improves VO2 max |

---

### Seven Fundamental Movement Patterns

These are the movements we use in daily life — exercises should replicate them:

| Movement | Daily Example |
|---|---|
| **Push** | Pushing a door open, pushups |
| **Pull** | Pulling a shutter down, pull-ups |
| **Squat** | Sitting down and getting up |
| **Hinge** | Bending to tie shoelaces |
| **Gait** | Walking and running |
| **Lunge** | Going up and down stairs |
| **Twist** | Turning to grab something or talk to someone |

---

### Muscle Movement Types

| Type | Description | Benefit |
|---|---|---|
| **Concentric** | Muscle contracts while shortening (lifting phase) | Powers the movement |
| **Isometric** | Muscle tension held with no length change | Stability |
| **Eccentric** | Muscle lengthens under load (lowering phase) | **Key driver of hypertrophy** |

**How muscle grows:** During eccentric movement, the muscle undergoes micro tears → body enters a **catabolic state** → healed with protein during sleep → muscle grows to handle future stress. Same process applies to **bone density**.

---

### Muscle Roles During Exercise

| Role | Function | Example (bicep curl) |
|---|---|---|
| **Agonist** | Primary mover | Biceps |
| **Synergist** | Assists the agonist to complete movement | Brachialis |
| **Antagonist** | Opposes the agonist for controlled movement | Triceps |
| **Stabilizer** | Stabilizes the body during the movement | Core muscles |

> Always maintain **mind-muscle connection** — be aware of which muscles are working during each exercise.

---

### Types of Equipment

| Type | Best For | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Free weights** (dumbbells, barbells) | Compound movements | More freedom of movement |
| **Resistance machines** | Isolated movements | Good for beginners; reduces injury risk |
| **Bodyweight** | Pushups, pull-ups, squats, lunges | No equipment needed |

---

### Compound vs. Isolated Movements

| Type | Description | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Compound** | Involves many muscle groups; activates CNS; burns more calories; triggers testosterone secretion | Squat, deadlift, bench press |
| **Isolated** | Targets a single muscle group; limited weight progression | Bicep curl, lateral raise |

**Progressive overload** (adding weight over time) is much more feasible with compound movements.

### Major Muscle Groups

#### Upper Body

**Chest:**
- Upper, middle, lower — each targeted differently

**Triceps:**
- Long head, lateral head (horseshoe look), medial head (underneath)

**Biceps:**
- Long head, short head (peak look), brachialis (arm thickness — often undertrained)

**Shoulders:**
- Anterior deltoid (front), lateral deltoid (side), posterior/rear deltoid (back)
- Upper trapezius — together give the 3D shoulder look

**Back:**
- Traps (upper, middle, lower — different fiber directions)
- Lats — give the V-taper look
- Rhomboids
- Deep back muscles — responsible for shoulder stability (most shoulder pain originates here)

> **Recommended:** Squat University (YouTube) by Dr. Aaron — best resource for injury prevention during lifts

**Forearms:**
- Brachioradialis (major muscle)
- Many muscles for pronation, supination, flexion, extension — train for grip strength

**Neck:**
- Levator Scapula, SCM — important for posture when working at a computer
- Stretch and strengthen for better posture

#### Lower Body

**Quads (Quadriceps):** 4 muscles at the front of the thigh

**Hamstrings:** Back of the thigh

**Glutes:**
- Gluteus Maximus — primary mover in squats/deadlifts
- Gluteus Medius — internal/external thigh rotation; weak glute med = stability issues

**Calves:**
- Gastrocnemius (outer), Soleus (inner)

**Adductors:** Inner thigh — pulls leg toward midline

#### Core (The Real Deal)

| Muscle | Location/Function |
|---|---|
| Rectus Abdominis | The "six-pack" muscle |
| External Oblique | Fiber runs top-down (sides) |
| Internal Oblique | Fiber runs upward (beneath external oblique) |
| **Transverse Abdominis** | Horizontal fibers — forms the "can walls" |
| **Erector Spinae** | Along the spine |
| **Diaphragm** | Top of the "can" |
| **Pelvic Floor** | Bottom of the "can" |
| Quadratus Lumborum, Multifidus, Rotators | Deep stabilizers |

The spine is the only structure connecting your upper and lower body. **Core muscles must be activated during any bending/lifting movement** to maintain a neutral spine and avoid lower back pain.

> **Self-learn:** Fundamental movement terminology | Planes of motion

### Exercise Library — Key Points Before Starting

1. **Proper form first** — always, to prevent injury
2. **Feel the muscle** — establish mind-muscle connection
3. **Full range of motion** — don't do half reps
4. **Controlled breathing** — inhale before lift (brace core), exhale on eccentric

### Core Exercises (Do These First — Every Session)

- McGill Big 3
- Planks
- Shoulder taps

Also engage core in all compound exercises.

### Exercise List by Muscle Group

#### Chest

Multiple exercises targeting upper, middle, and lower chest.

**Bench Press muscle roles:**
- Agonist: Pectoralis major
- Synergists: Triceps, anterior deltoid, serratus anterior

#### Back

- **Vertical pulls** (lat pulldown, pull-ups) → **add width**
- **Horizontal pulls** (rows) → **add thickness**

**Lat Pulldown breakdown:**
1. Scapular retraction and depression (stabilize shoulder)
2. Pull the weight down (lats as agonist; traps, rear delt, biceps as synergists)

**Rear Delt High Row:** Targets rear delt, rhomboids, middle traps — no lat involvement.

#### Shoulders

Focus on lateral raise for lateral delts.

#### Biceps

Two exercises are enough (you're already hitting biceps during back workouts):
1. **Incline dumbbell curl** — long head and short head
2. **Cross-body hammer curl** — brachialis

#### Triceps

Need both a stretch and a contraction exercise:
1. Overhead tricep extension (stretches long and lateral head)
2. Tricep pushdown (contraction — what the stretch can't achieve)
3. Reverse grip cable pushdown (medial head)

#### Forearms

Various exercises for grip strength → better compound lifts.

#### Legs

Leg exercises use more energy (bigger muscles). Proper form is critical — engage core throughout.

**Squats:**
- Prime movers: Quads and glutes
- Feet slightly flared, knees tracking over feet
- Core engaged, back squeezed, go as deep as possible
- All leg muscles involved

**Romanian Deadlift:**
- Focus: Hamstrings and glutes
- Key difference from squat: Don't move knees past feet — push glutes back to stretch hamstrings

#### Abs

Two variations only — you're already working core heavily in compound lifts:
1. Crunch variation
2. Leg raise

### Stretching

**Dynamic stretches (before workout):**
- Warm up blood flow around the target muscles
- Like warming up a rubber band before stretching it
- Choose stretches based on the muscle group for that day
- Always do dynamic stretches for the neck

**Static stretches (after workout):**
- Hold for 15–30 seconds
- Target the muscles worked that day

**Critical for desk workers:**
- **Rotator cuff activation** — shoulders always hunch toward the laptop; do rotator cuff work before every upper body session
- **Hip flexor stretches** — sitting keeps hip flexors contracted and overactive; stretch these daily

> **Self-learn:** Muscle imbalance | Overactive vs underactive muscles | How to strengthen underactive and stretch overactive muscles

---

### Workout Structure (Single Session)

1. Dynamic warm-up (light cardio + dynamic stretches — don't exhaust yourself)
2. Core activation and stabilization work
3. Target muscle workout — **compound lifts first**, then isolated
4. Cardio (based on goal)
5. Static stretching for recovery

---

### Sets, Reps, and Rest

| Goal | Sets | Reps | Rest |
|---|---|---|---|
| Endurance | 2–3 | 15–20 | 30–60 sec |
| **Hypertrophy** | **2–3** | **8–12** | **60–90 sec** |
| Strength | 3–5 | 3–5 | 2–5 min |

**For beginners:** Start with hypertrophy range. The 12th rep should be a genuine challenge.

**Progressive overload:** Once 12 reps feel easy, either:
- Increase reps to 15, then add weight and return to 12, **OR**
- Add weight directly and aim for 12

### Weekly Training Structure

#### Beginner (Focus on form, light weights)

- 1 muscle group per day, 2–3 exercises only
- Example: Chest Mon / Back Tue / Legs Wed / Rest / Shoulders Fri / Arms Sat / Rest

#### Intermediate — 4 Days/Week

**Push/Pull/Legs/Misc:**
- Push: Chest, shoulders, triceps
- Pull: Back, biceps
- Legs: Full leg session
- Misc: Lagging parts + cardio + abs

**Upper/Lower:**
- Upper → Lower → Rest → Upper → Lower → Rest + Rest

#### Busy — 3 Days/Week

- Legs / Push / Pull, **OR**
- Full Body / Upper / Lower

#### Very Busy — 2 Days/Week

- Full body on both days
- Prioritize compound movements, cover all body parts

> **Self-learn:** Training techniques — supersets, antagonist supersets, drop sets, etc.

### Ego and Injury

Don't compare yourself to others in the gym. Don't do **ego lifting** — lifting more than your form allows. Most disc injuries come from heavy squats and deadlifts with poor form. Focus on your own progress.

### Cardio and Energy Systems

The body uses different energy sources depending on activity duration and intensity:

**Anaerobic system** (no oxygen required):
- Used for sprinting and high-intensity bursts
- Fuel: ATP, creatine phosphate, glycogen
- Depleted in 2–3 minutes — that's why you can't sprint for long

**Aerobic system** (oxygen required):
- Used for sustained, lower-intensity activities
- Fuel: **Fat** — this is where fat burning happens

#### Cardio Training Zones

| Zone | % Max HR | Fuel Source | Activity |
|---|---|---|---|
| Zone 1 | 50–60% | Fat only | Brisk walk |
| **Zone 2** | **60–80%** | **Fat + glycogen** | Moderate jog — more calories in less time |
| Zone 3 / Anaerobic | > 80–90% | Glycogen (no fat) | Sprinting, HIIT |

> 10,000 steps/day keeps you in Zone 1, using fat as the primary fuel source. This is why daily walking leads to gradual, sustained weight loss.

**HIIT (High Intensity Interval Training):**
- 30 sec on (battle ropes, burpees, jumping jacks) / 30 sec rest × 10–15 min
- Improves VO2 max

**4×4 Protocol:**
- 4 min high intensity / 3 min rest × 4 rounds
- Graduate to this from HIIT

### Spot Fat Reduction: Myth

**You cannot choose where your body loses fat.** Your body decides. The only lever you control is **caloric deficit**. Be consistent — results will come in the long run.

2. Core + rotator cuff isometric exercises

4. Add cardio
5. Progressively improve weight and add exercises

No urgency. Keep improving.

## Video 4: Life Situations

### India and Protein

Indian diets are high in carbs and fat, low in protein. Educate your family on the importance of protein and fiber. **Get them on the same page** — sit together and talk about it.

---

### Traveling

- Prioritize protein and fiber in every meal
- Walk wherever possible — you don't have your car anyway
- Do bodyweight exercises in hotel rooms: pushups, pull-ups, squats, burpees, jumping jacks
- Use a resistance band for light resistance training
- Avoid junk food; enjoy local cuisine **without overeating**
- Carry protein bars, nuts, seeds as backup

---

### Reframing Your Relationship with Food

Food restriction leads to cravings and rebellion. Remember: in today's economy, you can get any food anytime. It's not about scarcity. Apply the same framework:

> **Joy of eating vs Guilt of eating**

If joy > guilt → eat it in moderation.
If guilt >= joy → skip it.

---

### Family Functions (Weddings, Receptions, Birthday Parties)

- Eat **protein and fiber before going** to the event
- At the event, have sweets/rice in moderation
- Be mindful — don't overeat

---

### Illness (Fever, Cold)

- Don't worry about diet targets during acute illness
- Focus on **recovery** — light foods, soups, broths
- Stay well hydrated
- Rest. Resume normal training once recovered.

---

### Skipping Workouts

Missing a session or two won't erase your gains. If you plan 150–200 workouts per year, 1–2 missed sessions are negligible.

- Be mindful of what you eat that day
- Keep moving in small ways
- **Resume as soon as things settle. Consistency is everything.**

---

### NEAT (Non-Exercise Activity Thermogenesis)

- Take kids to the park, grocery store, or on walks — spend extra calories while bonding
- Kids are naturally active — join them
- 10,000 steps is a great target, but 7,000–8,000 steps is still good and more sustainable
- Adjust the target to what you can consistently hit

---

### Learning in the Gym

You'll see people working out differently. Don't judge — **observe**. Note it down, research it later, and decide if it contradicts your existing knowledge. Be open to updating your understanding.

---

### Stress

Stress is a silent killer — it causes fat gain, poor recovery, and poor sleep.

- Focus on what you **can control**. Take action. Don't ruminate.
- Don't get overwhelmed by all the concepts in this series — go one step at a time.
- **Celebrate small wins.** Compound results follow consistent small wins.

---

### Sleep

Sleep is when the body recovers and muscles grow. It is the most important lifestyle factor.
