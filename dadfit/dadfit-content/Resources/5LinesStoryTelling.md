### The Five Line Story Method

This is the method I've been using to create almost all my story-driven videos. The only thing you have to do is write five lines.

1. **Situation** — The starting point. Where are we and what's happening? How does the world look like where we're in?
2. **Desire** — What does the character want? What does she want to obtain, or what does she want to reach?
3. **Conflict** — What gets in the way? Where's the tension, or what is an obstacle that makes it hard to reach the desire?
4. **Change** — What happens that shifts something? This could be a turning point in the story — something big or something small, or just a different way of seeing something.
5. **Result** — How does it end? What's the new reality and how is the situation different now from when we started?

Now this might all sound very simple and easy. But believe me when I say that simple is good with filmmaking. Because these five lines will help you to see the whole story before you get distracted by everything else. By gear. Transitions. Voiceovers. Endless color grading — you name it.

There are so many details and so many choices to be made. So if you nail these five lines first, you've got the emotional core of your story. It's like the life thread.

### Why It Works — The Lion King Example

I want to show you how even big Hollywood films use this structure. Let's use a story you definitely know: **The Lion King**.

- **Situation:** Simba is a young prince excited to one day rule the Pride Lands.
- **Desire:** He wants to prove he's brave and worthy of being a king.
- **Conflict:** After his father's death, Simba runs away, ashamed and afraid to face his past.
- **Change:** Simba grows up and, with help from his friends and a vision from his father, returns to confront Scar and take his place as a king.
- **Result:** He restores balance to the Pride Lands and steps fully into who he was meant to be.

Obviously there are missing quite some details to make the story a little bit richer, but it *is* the whole story. And the funny thing is, once I started using this method, I started seeing this in every movie I watched. And probably you will too now. Next time when you watch a movie, you will probably see the point where the conflict is introduced or where the desire is introduced. So I'm sorry if no movie will be the same anymore for you.

But it's not just a beginner's trick. It's just how almost every story works.

### Breaking Down Each Line

**Situation** — Where we are is very important because it gives the viewer a clear starting point of where we are, what's happening, and who's in the story. They will know the world that we're in and what we're actually talking about.

**Desire** — This really shows the goal or the need that's driving the story. This usually sparks the curiosity of the audience, and this is the part where people will lean more into the story and start to care about whether you're getting the thing or not. The audience will start to feel invested in the story. And maybe the thing the main character wants is something they want as well.

**Conflict** — This introduces tension or an obstacle. And this is really something that stories need. Because if there's no tension, if there's no obstacle, if there's no challenge, there's not really a reason to keep watching for the audience.

**Change** — This really marks a turning point in the story. It could be an insight or an action or anything that shifts everything. It shows growth and gives the audience the feeling of movement forward.

**Result** — This should really give your audience a satisfying takeaway, a feeling of closure, an ending. And it should be something that ties everything of the whole story together. It shows how the world, the situation, or the character is different from the starting point.

Together, these five lines are just how we naturally process every story.

### From Five Lines to a Full Video

I want you to know that these five lines aren't your whole video. They are some sort of launchpad. You can use them to kick off your story and start building from your five lines, or you can use them to come back at any time when your project is overwhelming you — to take a look again at your five lines and see if the things you are building in your story connect back to one of your five lines.

Now I'm going to show you how one of my own YouTube videos that's over eight minutes long can be distilled down to just five lines.

**Five lines:**

> I'm a filmmaker from the Netherlands and I've loved making videos since I was a kid. My dream was to be a YouTuber and sharing stories from the heart, not just for clients. But every time I tried to start, self-doubt and overthinking pulled me back. When I let go of the YouTube dream, I finally realized I was focusing on the wrong reasons and I just wanted to create stories. So I finally sat down and started, and this video is the result, and that first step pushed me to go all in on this YouTube journey.

That was basically the whole story of my first YouTube video in five lines. And now the cool thing is, when you can do it with five lines, you can do it with ten as well.

**Ten lines:**

> I'm a filmmaker from the Netherlands and I've loved making videos since I was a kid. My dream was to be a YouTuber and sharing stories from the heart, not just for clients. But every time I tried to start, self-doubt and overthinking pulled me back. My mind kept yelling, you can't go from making ads for big companies to making YouTube videos. Those fears became excuses: I'm too late and too old, I don't have enough skills. Gear. Security. So I let go of the big YouTube dream and at first it felt good and I felt free. But then the same question kept coming back: why did I actually want to do YouTube all the time? When I stripped away views, subscribers and money, I realized it's about the joy of creating and sharing stories. That realization made me push through self-doubt, focus on the process, and the video you're watching right now is the result of that. It wasn't easy, but I already learned so much, and it even made me go all in on YouTube and share every lesson along the way.

Going from five lines to ten lines doesn't change the core of the story. It just adds more richness and more details. I could show you the 20-line version now as well, but I think you get the point. I could continue showing you more lines until I show you the whole video. **So if you can do five lines, you can expand and expand more and create a whole story.**
