# Landing Page Structure — Copy Formulas & Examples

Each section below includes the formula, character guidance, and worked examples across multiple niches.

---

## Section 1: The Hook

The first thing visible on a phone screen. One job: make the reader feel *seen*.

### Hook Type A — Frustration Hook

**Formula:**
> Feeling frustrated that **[specific bad outcome]** even though you **[effort the person is already making]**?

**Rules:**
- Name the paradox: they're doing the right thing but not getting the result
- Use the word "even though" — it validates their effort and deepens the sting
- Keep under 15 words for headline, 25 for subline variant

**Examples:**
| Niche | Hook |
|-------|------|
| Fitness | "Feeling frustrated that you're not losing weight even though you work out every week?" |
| Finance | "Feeling frustrated that your investments aren't growing even though you save consistently every month?" |
| Marketing | "Feeling frustrated that your ads aren't converting even though you're spending real money?" |
| Sleep | "Feeling frustrated that you wake up exhausted even though you go to bed on time?" |
| Cybersecurity | "Feeling frustrated that your business still feels exposed even though you've invested in IT?" |

---

### Hook Type B — Results Hook (Readiness Sentence)

**Formula:**
> Are you ready to **[specific, tangible desired outcome]**?

**Rules:**
- The outcome must be specific enough to be believable but aspirational enough to create desire
- "Ready" implies self-doubt — the reader thinks: *Am I? I'm not sure. Let me find out.*
- Works best when the result is a clear milestone (not a vague improvement)

**Examples:**
| Niche | Hook |
|-------|------|
| Running | "Are you ready to run your first marathon?" |
| Relationships | "Are you ready to have the relationship of your dreams?" |
| Cybersecurity | "Are you ready to protect your business from a cyber attack?" |
| Sleep | "Are you ready to get 7+ hours of deep, restorative sleep every night?" |
| Lead gen | "Are you ready to generate 10 qualified leads a week on autopilot?" |

---

**Choosing between A and B:**
- Frustration hook → audience is already trying, feeling stuck, wants to diagnose *why*
- Results hook → audience is aspirational, hasn't started yet, wants to know if they're ready

## Section 2: The Subheading

**Formula:**
> Answer **[N]** questions to find out **[why frustration is happening / whether you're ready]** and **[what to do about it]**.

**Standard variant:**
> "Answer 15 questions to find out why you're experiencing this and exactly what to do about it."

**Customized variant (pair with frustration hook):**
> "Answer 15 questions to diagnose what's blocking your results — and get a personalized roadmap."

**Customized variant (pair with results hook):**
> "Answer 15 questions to discover how ready you are — and the exact next steps to get there."

**Rules:**
- Always state the number of questions (creates expectation, reduces friction)
- Always promise an outcome beyond just the score (diagnosis + recommendation)
- Keep to 1–2 sentences max

## Section 3: Value Proposition

**Formula:**
> Take this assessment so we can **measure and improve** your:
> 1. **[Area 1]**
> 2. **[Area 2]**
> 3. **[Area 3]**

**Rules:**
- Exactly 3 pillars — not 2, not 4 (3 is the cognitive sweet spot)
- Each pillar should be something the audience *wants* to improve, not just something you assess
- Use nouns that feel tangible: "sleep environment" not "your sleeping conditions"
- Optional: add 1 sentence intro ("People who improve these three areas typically see X")

**Examples by niche:**

| Niche | Pillar 1 | Pillar 2 | Pillar 3 |
|-------|----------|----------|----------|
| Sleep | Sleep environment | Sleep routine | Sleep nutrition |
| Fitness / weight loss | Nutrition habits | Training consistency | Recovery & rest |
| Lead generation | Lead capture strategy | Lead qualification | Follow-up system |
| Cybersecurity | Access control | Threat awareness | Incident response |
| Marketing / ads | Targeting & audience | Creative & messaging | Funnel & landing pages |
| Executive coaching | Strategic clarity | Leadership communication | Team performance |

## Section 4: Credibility

Three credibility components. All three increase trust at different levels.

### 4a. Creator Bio
**Formula:**
> **[Name]** is **[role/title]** who has **[specific achievement or track record]**.

**Example:**
> "James Harrington is a growth strategist who has helped 200+ B2B companies build lead generation systems that consistently convert."

### 4b. Research / Data Point
**Formula:**
> **[Stat]%** of **[audience]** struggle with **[problem]** — yet only **[smaller stat]%** know why.

**Example:**
> "82% of small business owners report inconsistent lead flow — yet only 11% have a documented lead generation system."

**Tips:**
- Use real, citable statistics where possible
- If no external stat exists, use internal data: "In our work with 500 clients, we found…"
- A Harvard / McKinsey / industry report citation dramatically increases perceived authority

### 4c. Social Proof (optional but powerful)
Short quote from a result-getter, or a count: "Taken by 4,200+ professionals"

## Section 5: CTA (Call to Action)

**The 5 micro-copy elements** (all must appear near the CTA button):

| Element | Purpose | Example copy |
|---------|---------|--------------|
| **Action verb** | Tells them what to do | "Start the quiz" |
| **Time commitment** | Removes friction | "Takes only 3 minutes" |
| **Free** | Eliminates risk | "100% free" |
| **Immediate value** | Promises instant reward | "Get your results instantly" |
| **Personalization** | Makes it feel tailored | "Receive personalized recommendations" |

**Full CTA block example:**
```
[Start My Free Assessment →]

Takes just 3 minutes · Completely free · Get instant personalized recommendations
```

**Button copy options (choose one):**
- "Start the Quiz →"
- "Get My Score →"  
- "Find Out Where I Stand →"
- "Start My Free Assessment →"
- "Discover My Readiness Score →"

**Rules:**
- One button only. No competing CTAs, no nav bar links.
- Repeat the CTA button at the bottom of the page (after credibility section)
- Button color must be the brand's highest-contrast accent color

## Page Layout Order (Scroll Map)

```
┌─────────────────────────────┐
│  HOOK (headline)            │  ← Above the fold on mobile
│  SUBHEADING                 │  ← Above the fold on mobile
│  CTA BUTTON (primary)       │  ← Above the fold on mobile
├─────────────────────────────┤
│  VALUE PROPOSITION          │  ← First scroll
│  (3 pillars, icons/visuals) │
├─────────────────────────────┤
│  CREDIBILITY                │  ← Second scroll
│  (bio + stat + social proof)│
├─────────────────────────────┤
│  CTA BUTTON (repeated)      │  ← Final scroll
│  Micro-copy reminders       │
│  (3 min · free · instant)   │
└─────────────────────────────┘
```

**Conversion benchmark:** A well-built page of this structure should convert 20–40% of visitors into quiz starters.
